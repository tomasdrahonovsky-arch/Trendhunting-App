## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between spontaneity and structure, between the desire for authentic experience and the systems that constrain it. The British "spontaneity crisis" — where the average person hasn't done anything unplanned in 80 days — speaks to a deeper malaise: we've optimised ourselves into paralysis. Meanwhile, the wine industry's structural collapse in Bordeaux contrasts sharply with resilience in prestige regions, suggesting consumers are bifurcating between accessible everyday choices and investment-grade experiences, with little appetite for the middle ground.

The emergence of death doulas among young people signals a generation reclaiming agency over life's most uncontrollable moment. This isn't morbidity — it's a rejection of institutional mediation in favour of human-centred care. Similarly, activist groups "liberating" food from supermarkets represent a moral recalibration around waste and access that mainstream brands cannot ignore.

In hospitality, we see the continued rise of hybrid spaces — bakery-bar-bistros, brewery-pizzerias — that refuse categorical boundaries. These venues understand that modern consumers don't want to choose between morning coffee and evening wine; they want spaces that evolve with their day.

The Erewhon Reserve membership tier ($300/month for grocery shopping) crystallises a troubling truth: wellness has become a luxury good, and exclusivity is now a feature, not a bug. For non-alcoholic beverages, this creates both opportunity and obligation — to democratise functional benefits while resisting the pull toward premium-only positioning.

The cultural moment demands brands that can hold paradox: spontaneous yet intentional, accessible yet special, functional yet pleasurable. The winners will be those who understand that control and release are not opposites but dance partners.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Spontaneity Deficit

**What happened:** Research commissioned by Cruzcampo found the average Brit hasn't done anything spontaneous in 80 days, with 28% rarely accepting last-minute plans and consumers now requiring 10 days' advance notice for social commitments.

**Why it is a signal:** This quantifies the over-scheduling epidemic. The pandemic normalised hyper-planning; it hasn't unwound. Social connection now requires administrative overhead that many find exhausting.

**Societal layer:** Control / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Position NA drinks as enablers of spontaneous socialising — "yes" drinks that don't require next-day recovery planning. Create venues and occasions designed for impromptu gatherings rather than reservations.

**What to monitor:** Calendar app usage data; same-day booking rates at hospitality venues; growth of "low-commitment" social formats.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Death Reclaimed by the Young

**What happened:** Increasing numbers of young people are training as death doulas, driven by personal experiences of navigating loss without institutional support and a desire to humanise end-of-life care.

**Why it is a signal:** Gen Z is actively rejecting the medicalisation and professionalisation of life's most intimate moments. This represents a broader movement toward human-centred care and away from institutional gatekeeping.

**Societal layer:** Spirituality / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Opportunity for beverages and spaces designed around grief, remembrance, and difficult conversations. Consider ritual drinks for wakes, memorial gatherings, and "death cafes" — spaces where mortality can be discussed openly.

**What to monitor:** Death cafe attendance; hospice volunteer demographics; funeral industry disruption; grief-focused community spaces.

---

### SIGNAL 3: The Bifurcation of Wine Value

**What happened:** French vineyard prices fell 2.9% overall, with Bordeaux collapsing 23.8% and Margaux down 43%, while Burgundy rose 3.9% and Champagne held steady. Prestige and everyday are thriving; the middle is dying.

**Why it is a signal:** The "squeezed middle" phenomenon has reached agricultural assets. Consumers want either accessible everyday options or investment-grade prestige — nothing in between justifies its price.

**Societal layer:** Status / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands must choose their lane clearly: either radically accessible (price, distribution, occasion) or genuinely premium (provenance, craft, scarcity). Avoid the muddled middle where Bordeaux is drowning.

**What to monitor:** Price elasticity in NA categories; emergence of "investment-grade" NA products; discount retailer NA expansion.

---

### SIGNAL 4: Wellness as Gated Community

**What happened:** Erewhon launched "Reserve" membership tiers, with the top tier costing $300/month for grocery shopping privileges, including early access to products and exclusive events.

**Why it is a signal:** Health and wellness have completed their transformation from universal aspiration to luxury positioning. Access to "clean" food and supplements is now explicitly stratified by income.

**Societal layer:** Status / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Tension point for NA brands: the wellness association drives premiumisation, but risks alienating mass-market consumers who feel excluded from health. Consider "accessible wellness" positioning that democratises functional benefits.

**What to monitor:** Wellness brand pricing trajectories; emergence of "dupe" functional beverages; backlash discourse around wellness gatekeeping.

---

### SIGNAL 5: The Hybrid Venue Ascendant

**What happened:** Multiple new venues blur traditional categories: No. Forty Nine combines bakery-bar-bistro; Siren's Tap Yard integrates craft beer with artisan pizza. These spaces serve customers from morning coffee through evening drinks.

**Why it is a signal:** Consumers reject categorical thinking about venues. They want spaces that accommodate their full day and evolving needs, not single-purpose destinations requiring multiple trips.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages must work across dayparts and occasions within single venues. Design products and serves that transition from morning ritual to afternoon refreshment to evening social without feeling out of place.

**What to monitor:** Multi-concept venue openings; daypart crossover in beverage sales; "all-day" menu proliferation.

---

### SIGNAL 6: Food Liberation as Moral Act

**What happened:** Activist group "Take Back Power" is systematically "liberating" food from supermarkets, reframing theft as ethical redistribution in response to food waste and access inequality.

**Why it is a signal:** A generation raised on climate anxiety is developing its own moral framework around consumption, waste, and access — one that doesn't align with property rights as traditionally understood.

**Societal layer:** Rebellion / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brands must demonstrate genuine commitment to waste reduction and access equity. Performative sustainability will be called out; radical transparency about supply chain and waste will be rewarded.

**What to monitor:** Food waste activism; "freegan" community growth; brand boycotts over waste practices; redistribution app usage.

---

### SIGNAL 7: The Airport Pint Under Threat

**What happened:** Airlines including Ryanair, EasyJet, and Jet2 are calling for restrictions on airport alcohol sales, including early morning bans, following increased disruptive passenger incidents.

**Why it is a signal:** The "rules don't apply" psychology of travel is being challenged. Airports may become the next frontier for moderation culture, creating significant occasion opportunity for NA alternatives.

**Societal layer:** Control / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Massive opportunity in travel retail for sophisticated NA options that preserve the ritual of "holiday starts here" without the behavioural risks. Partner with airlines and airports proactively.

**What to monitor:** Airport alcohol policy changes; airline NA beverage offerings; travel retail NA sales data; duty-free category evolution.

---

### SIGNAL 8: Single-Serve Format Momentum

**What happened:** Australian Vintage's Poco Vino single-serve wine format is expanding aggressively, with a Hong Kong tram takeover and integrated lifestyle activations across fitness, beauty, and wellness communities.

**Why it is a signal:** Portion control and convenience are converging. Single-serve formats remove the commitment anxiety of opening a full bottle while enabling precise consumption management.

**Societal layer:** Control / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Single-serve NA formats should be prioritised, particularly for trial and on-the-go occasions. The format itself communicates moderation and intentionality.

**What to monitor:** Single-serve format growth across beverage categories; convenience store NA range expansion; portion-controlled packaging innovation.

---

### SIGNAL 9: The Fine Wine Exchange Economy

**What happened:** Jeroboams launched an Exchange platform enabling customers to sell wine from their collections, arguing that "a modern fine wine merchant can't just sell people wine" — they must facilitate the full ownership lifecycle.

**Why it is a signal:** Ownership models are evolving toward liquidity and circulation rather than accumulation. Consumers want to participate in markets, not just make purchases.

**Societal layer:** Status / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Consider how NA brands might enable secondary markets, trading, or collection dynamics. Limited releases with resale value could build investment-grade positioning.

**What to monitor:** Beverage resale platforms; collector community formation around NA products; limited release sell-through and secondary pricing.

---

### SIGNAL 10: Celebrity Wine Moments as Cultural Currency

**What happened:** NBA player Josh Hart brought a glass of red wine to a post-game press conference, sparking viral speculation about the specific bottle and debate about whether this was celebration or unprofessionalism.

**Why it is a signal:** Wine (and by extension, sophisticated beverages) functions as cultural signifier in unexpected contexts. The "what's in the glass" detective work shows deep engagement with beverage as identity marker.

**Societal layer:** Status / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands need their own "what's in the glass" moments — products distinctive enough to spark curiosity and conversation when spotted in unexpected contexts.

**What to monitor:** Celebrity beverage choices; social media beverage identification threads; product placement in sports and entertainment contexts.

---

### SIGNAL 11: Riot Grrrl Nostalgia and Anti-Hygiene Aesthetics

**What happened:** The 30th anniversary of "Dirty Girls" documentary is prompting reflection on 1990s riot grrrl culture, including its deliberate rejection of conventional beauty and hygiene standards as feminist statement.

**Why it is a signal:** Cyclical nostalgia for anti-establishment aesthetics suggests continued appetite for brands that reject polish and perfection in favour of authenticity and deliberate imperfection.

**Societal layer:** Rebellion / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Space exists for NA brands that reject the clean, clinical wellness aesthetic in favour of something rawer and more countercultural. Not every NA drink needs to be aspirational.

**What to monitor:** DIY aesthetic resurgence; "anti-wellness" wellness; punk and riot grrrl cultural references in marketing; deliberately imperfect brand presentations.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The 48-Hour Bar

**Hypothesis:** A venue that only accepts same-day bookings (no reservations more than 48 hours ahead) will attract spontaneity-seeking consumers and create a distinctive positioning in an over-planned hospitality landscape.

**Method:** Partner with an existing bar or create a pop-up that operates on a strict 48-hour booking window. Feature NA cocktails prominently as "yes drinks" that enable spontaneous socialising without next-day consequences. Track booking patterns, customer demographics, and repeat visit rates. Measure social media sentiment around the "spontaneity" positioning.

**Success metric:** 70% capacity utilisation within 60 days; 40% of customers citing "spontaneity concept" as visit driver; measurable increase in NA beverage share versus comparable venues.

---

### Experiment 2: The Grief Menu

**Hypothesis:** A curated selection of NA beverages designed specifically for difficult conversations — grief, loss, life transitions — will find audience among the growing death-positive movement and fill an unmet emotional occasion need.

**Method:** Develop a small range of NA drinks with names, flavours, and serving rituals designed around comfort and contemplation rather than celebration. Partner with death cafes, hospices, and grief counsellors for distribution and feedback. Create accompanying ritual guides. Test at memorial events and in dedicated "difficult conversation" spaces.

**Success metric:** Adoption by 10+ death cafes or grief-focused venues within 6 months; qualitative feedback indicating emotional resonance; organic social sharing of ritual moments.

---

### Experiment 3: The Travel Ritual Kit

**Hypothesis:** A premium NA beverage kit designed specifically for airport/travel occasions — preserving the "holiday starts here" ritual without alcohol — will capture demand as airlines push for drinking restrictions.

**Method:** Create a travel-sized NA aperitif or sparkling option with premium packaging designed for airport purchase. Partner with one airport retailer for exclusive trial. Position explicitly as the sophisticated alternative for travellers who want the ritual without the risk. Track sales velocity, customer feedback, and airline/airport partnership interest.

**Success metric:** Sales velocity exceeding comparable alcoholic products in test location; partnership enquiries from additional airports or airlines; social media documentation of "travel ritual" moments.
