## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between efficiency and authenticity, as consumers navigate economic pressure while seeking genuine connection and identity expression. The drinks industry is bifurcating: higher-ABV RTDs gain traction for "efficient" pre-going-out rituals, while the no/low category continues its parallel rise for mindful occasions. This isn't contradiction—it's deliberate occasion-switching by consumers who refuse to be categorised.

The economic squeeze is reshaping behaviour at every level. Record numbers of UK renters are crowdfunding basic bills, youth unemployment is soaring, and Australian wine tourism faces collapse as fuel costs make regional visits prohibitive. Yet within this constraint, we see creative adaptation: digital cellar doors extending relationships beyond physical visits, walking dates replacing expensive dinners, and dates (the fruit) surging as an affordable, health-conscious snack alternative.

Identity politics are intensifying. British Palestinians report feeling unable to display cultural symbols publicly, while queer nightlife photography grapples with the tension between documentation and surveillance. These aren't peripheral concerns—they signal how public spaces and consumption contexts are becoming increasingly coded and contested.

The health-wellness nexus continues evolving beyond simple "clean" messaging. Cold exposure for weight loss, fibre awareness driving date consumption, and a Brazilian tree compound showing multi-vector COVID efficacy all point toward consumers seeking functional, evidence-based interventions rather than vague wellness promises.

For non-alcoholic beverage brands, the strategic imperative is clear: build for occasion flexibility, respect economic constraints, and create products that serve identity expression without requiring explanation. The winning brands will be those that understand consumption as a form of communication—and give consumers something worth saying.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Efficiency Paradox in Drinking Occasions

**What happened:** Southern Glazer's reports higher-ABV RTDs growing alongside no/low options, with consumers using stronger drinks for "efficient" pre-going-out consumption and lighter options for pacing.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents the death of the "type of drinker" persona. Consumers now switch modes based on occasion, budget, and desired outcome—treating alcohol as a tool rather than an identity marker.

**Societal layer:** Control / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands must position for specific occasions rather than lifestyle segments. The same consumer buying your sophisticated NA aperitif on Tuesday may want a quick, functional option on Friday. Portfolio breadth and occasion-specific marketing become essential.

**What to monitor:** RTD format innovation in NA space; pre-event consumption patterns among 25-35 demographic; emergence of "efficiency" language in beverage marketing.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Digital Cellar Doors as Relationship Infrastructure

**What happened:** Sussex producer Artelium launched a digital cellar door enabling global direct-to-consumer sales, wine club memberships, and curated collections—extending the physical visit into ongoing relationship.

**Why it is a signal:** This moves beyond e-commerce into relationship architecture. The cellar door experience becomes a gateway rather than destination, with digital tools maintaining emotional connection across distance.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands with physical touchpoints (taprooms, pop-ups, events) should build digital infrastructure that captures and extends those moments. The goal isn't just sales—it's creating ongoing membership-style relationships that survive between encounters.

**What to monitor:** Wine club membership retention rates; post-visit conversion windows; emergence of "digital cellar door" as category term.

---

### SIGNAL 3: Economic Constraint Reshaping Access to Experience

**What happened:** Australian wine tourism faces collapse as fuel crisis makes regional visits unaffordable; UK renters crowdfund basic bills at record rates; youth unemployment soars.

**Why it is a signal:** Experience-based consumption is becoming a luxury. The "experience economy" thesis assumed discretionary spending—that assumption is breaking down for large segments of the population.

**Societal layer:** Status / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium NA brands must reckon with accessibility. Consider urban pop-ups that don't require travel, smaller format/price point options, and experiences that deliver status without requiring significant financial or logistical investment.

**What to monitor:** Fuel price trends; regional tourism booking data; emergence of "micro-experience" formats in hospitality.

---

### SIGNAL 4: Dates as the New Functional Snack

**What happened:** Dates are surging in popularity driven by viral recipes, fibre awareness, and demand for alternatives to ultra-processed foods—repositioning from niche ingredient to mainstream snack.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents the maturation of "functional food" from supplement-adjacent products to whole foods with inherent benefits. Consumers want function without processing.

**Societal layer:** Control / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages should explore whole-food functional ingredients with cultural heritage and inherent health benefits. Date-based drinks, date syrup as sweetener, or date-forward flavour profiles could capture this momentum while delivering genuine function.

**What to monitor:** Date import volumes; recipe search trends; emergence of date-based beverage products in Middle Eastern and health-focused markets.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Walking Dates and Low-Stakes Social Formats

**What happened:** Walking dates are being positioned as superior to dinner dates—lower cost, less pressure, easier exit, and better for introverts who struggle with prolonged eye contact.

**Why it is a signal:** Social formats are being redesigned around anxiety reduction and economic constraint. The formal, high-investment social occasion is losing ground to flexible, low-commitment alternatives.

**Societal layer:** Mental Health / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Create products and formats designed for movement and flexibility. Portable, resealable, single-serve options that work for walking, parks, and informal gatherings. Consider partnerships with walking groups, outdoor spaces, and low-key social venues.

**What to monitor:** Dating app feature development; park and outdoor venue beverage policies; growth of walking/hiking social groups.

### SIGNAL 6: Queer Nightlife Documentation Tension

**What happened:** A new MACK photo book explores the tension between documenting queer nightlife for preservation and the intrusion/surveillance risk that photography creates in spaces meant to be private.

**Why it is a signal:** Safe spaces are becoming contested territory between visibility (for community building and historical record) and privacy (for protection). This tension will shape how brands engage with subcultural communities.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brands seeking to engage queer nightlife must navigate consent and privacy carefully. Support without surveillance. Sponsorship without documentation requirements. Let communities control their own narratives.

**What to monitor:** Venue photography policies; community response to brand partnerships; evolution of "safe space" definitions in nightlife.

### SIGNAL 7: Travel Retail as Global Taste Laboratory

**What happened:** TFWA Asia Pacific conference highlighted connectivity as enduring human desire despite geopolitical shocks, with younger Asian middle class driving adventure-seeking despite global instability.

**Why it is a signal:** Travel retail remains a unique space where consumers encounter products outside their home market context. Despite disruption, the desire to explore and discover persists—and travel retail is where that discovery often happens.

**Societal layer:** Status / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Travel retail represents an underexploited channel for NA beverages. Travellers seeking novel experiences, often in moderation-friendly contexts (flying, business travel), are ideal NA consumers. Duty-free positioning could accelerate global brand building.

**What to monitor:** NA beverage presence in travel retail; airport lounge beverage programmes; airline beverage service evolution.

### SIGNAL 8: Mandatory Health Warnings Expanding

**What happened:** South Korea will require drink-driving warnings on all alcohol from November 2026, adding to existing pregnancy and health risk warnings—with moves toward more graphic visual messaging.

**Why it is a signal:** Regulatory pressure on alcohol is intensifying globally, with warnings becoming more prominent and specific. This creates both challenge (category stigma) and opportunity (NA alternatives positioned as responsible choice).

**Societal layer:** Control / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands should prepare for a regulatory environment that increasingly distinguishes them from alcoholic alternatives. Consider how packaging and marketing can emphasise the absence of these warning requirements as implicit benefit.

**What to monitor:** Warning label legislation globally; graphic warning image adoption; consumer response to warning prominence.

### SIGNAL 9: Multi-Vector Natural Compounds

**What happened:** Brazilian researchers found galloylquinic acids from Copaifera lucens attack COVID-19 across multiple mechanisms—spike protein, immune evasion enzyme, and reproduction—while showing anti-inflammatory effects.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a shift from single-target interventions to multi-mechanism approaches, and from synthetic to naturally-derived compounds. Consumers increasingly expect functional claims to be specific and evidence-based.

**Societal layer:** Control / Spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional NA beverages should move beyond vague wellness claims toward specific, multi-benefit positioning backed by research. Ingredient stories that combine traditional use with modern validation will resonate.

**What to monitor:** Functional ingredient research pipelines; regulatory evolution for health claims; consumer literacy around mechanism-of-action language.

### SIGNAL 10: Identity Expression Under Pressure

**What happened:** British Palestinians report feeling unable to wear cultural symbols or display Arabic jewellery publicly, describing a climate where they feel treated as suspects rather than community members.

**Why it is a signal:** Public identity expression is becoming riskier for certain communities. Consumption choices—including beverages—become one of few "safe" ways to express cultural identity when visible symbols feel dangerous.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Belonging / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Beverages with cultural heritage (Middle Eastern, North African, South Asian) may serve as identity expression for diaspora communities. Authenticity and respect in sourcing and storytelling become essential—appropriation will be rejected.

**What to monitor:** Diaspora community consumption patterns; cultural beverage brand emergence; social media discourse around cultural expression and safety.

### SIGNAL 11: Provence Beyond Rosé

**What happened:** Elizabeth Gabay MW's 40-year retrospective emphasised that Provence was historically known for reds and whites, with rosé dominance being relatively recent—and the region's diversity now being rediscovered.

**Why it is a signal:** Category assumptions are being challenged. Regions and products become typecast, but consumer sophistication and producer ambition are pushing beyond these limitations.

**Societal layer:** Status / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands should resist being typecast as "the thing you drink when you're not drinking." Build category depth—different occasions, flavour profiles, complexity levels. Avoid becoming a one-note alternative.

**What to monitor:** NA product line extensions; occasion-specific NA positioning; emergence of NA "connoisseurship" language.

---

### SIGNAL 12: Climate as Public Health Emergency

**What happened:** WHO-convened commission recommends declaring climate crisis a "public health emergency of international concern"—the same designation used for COVID-19—arguing millions of deaths could be prevented.

**Why it is a signal:** Climate is being reframed from environmental issue to immediate health threat. This shifts the conversation from future generations to present-day bodies and creates new urgency around sustainable consumption.

**Societal layer:** Control / Spirituality / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Sustainability claims will face increasing scrutiny as climate becomes health-coded. Greenwashing risk intensifies. Brands must demonstrate genuine environmental commitment or face backlash from consumers who now see climate as personal health issue.

**What to monitor:** Climate-health messaging in consumer communications; carbon labelling adoption; consumer willingness to pay premium for verified sustainability.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: Occasion-Switching Portfolio Test

**Hypothesis:** Consumers who purchase NA beverages for mindful occasions will also purchase from the same brand for "efficient" pre-social occasions if offered appropriate format and positioning—increasing customer lifetime value by 40%+.

**Method:** Develop two distinct SKUs from single brand: (1) sophisticated, slow-sipping format for mindful moments, (2) portable, quick-consumption format positioned for pre-event energy/preparation. Test with existing customer base through targeted offers. Track cross-purchase behaviour and occasion self-reporting.

**Success metric:** 25%+ of customers purchasing both formats within 90 days; average order value increase of 30%+; qualitative feedback confirming distinct occasion use.

---

### Experiment 2: Digital Relationship Extension Programme

**Hypothesis:** Consumers who experience an NA brand through physical touchpoint (event, pop-up, tasting) will maintain purchasing relationship 3x longer if enrolled in digital membership programme with curated content and exclusive access.

**Method:** At next 10 physical activations, offer attendees enrollment in "Founder's Circle" digital programme including: monthly virtual tasting, early access to new products, behind-the-scenes content, and member-only purchasing windows. Compare 12-month retention and purchase frequency against control group (standard email list).

**Success metric:** 60%+ enrollment rate at events; 3x purchase frequency vs. control; 50%+ 12-month retention in programme.

---

### Experiment 3: Walking Date Partnership Activation

**Hypothesis:** Positioning NA beverages as ideal for low-stakes, movement-based social occasions (walking dates, park hangs, hiking meetups) will drive trial among anxiety-conscious younger consumers who avoid traditional bar/restaurant contexts.

**Method:** Partner with 3 walking/hiking social groups and 2 dating apps to provide branded portable NA beverages for organised walking events. Include QR code linking to "Walking Date Guide" content. Track redemption, social sharing, and subsequent direct purchase behaviour.

**Success metric:** 500+ event participants over 8 weeks; 15%+ conversion to direct purchase within 30 days; qualitative feedback confirming anxiety-reduction positioning resonance.
