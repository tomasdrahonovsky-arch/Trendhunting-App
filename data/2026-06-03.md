## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a fundamental tension between authenticity and performance, between heritage and reinvention. The drinks industry is navigating this through two distinct strategies: brands like Garage Beer are explicitly rejecting manufactured culture in favour of genuine participation in existing cultural moments, while heritage houses like González Byass and Château d'Yquem are monetising their archives as authenticity becomes a tradeable asset.

The hospitality sector faces a structural crisis. Operating costs have risen 48% over a decade, with training costs up 70% while only one in four staff survive 90 days. This isn't a temporary squeeze—it's a fundamental reshaping of what hospitality can economically deliver. The winners will be those who achieve operational efficiency without sacrificing experience.

Meanwhile, a generational fear of "cringe" is reshaping social behaviour. Gen Z's constant awareness of being recorded has created paralysis around enthusiasm and public expression. This has profound implications for how beverages are consumed socially—the spontaneous, uninhibited moments that alcohol traditionally facilitated are becoming culturally fraught.

The body is emerging as a site of both optimisation and anxiety. From "scrotox" to weight-loss drugs preventing knee replacements, we're seeing a medicalisation of appearance and function that extends to how people think about what they consume. Functional benefits are no longer nice-to-have—they're expected.

For non-alcoholic beverage brands, the opportunity lies in creating products and experiences that facilitate authentic connection without the social risk of embarrassment, that deliver functional benefits without clinical sterility, and that honour heritage while remaining culturally current. The brands that understand this moment will build lasting relevance; those that don't will become noise.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Anti-Manufactured Culture Stance

**What happened:** Garage Beer CEO Andy Sauer explicitly stated "We're not trying to manufacture culture; we're participating in it with people we genuinely respect," positioning the brand's summer campaign as organic cultural participation rather than top-down marketing.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a strategic shift from brands creating culture to brands earning permission to participate in existing cultural moments. It's a direct response to consumer fatigue with performative brand activism and forced relevance.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / authenticity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands must identify genuine cultural spaces where they can participate credibly rather than attempting to manufacture their own movements. Partnerships should emerge from shared values, not media reach.

**What to monitor:** Consumer sentiment around brand partnerships; backlash velocity when brands are perceived as inauthentic; which cultural spaces remain resistant to brand participation.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Heritage as Liquid Asset

**What happened:** González Byass is auctioning bottles from its historic archive dating to 1846 through Christie's, while Stalin's 40,000-bottle collection will fund a Georgian wine school. Château d'Yquem restored 1890s bottles found under a chapel floor.

**Why it is a signal:** Provenance and authenticity are becoming monetisable assets. Heritage brands are recognising their archives as both cultural capital and revenue streams, while collectors increasingly value story and traceability over pure rarity.

**Societal layer:** Status / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands lack historical archives but can create future heritage through documented provenance, limited releases with traceable origins, and partnerships with established cultural institutions.

**What to monitor:** Auction prices for documented-provenance bottles; growth of authentication technologies; collector interest in non-traditional categories.

---

### SIGNAL 3: The Cringe Paralysis

**What happened:** Gen Z is experiencing widespread fear of public enthusiasm and self-expression due to constant recording risk. A subsection of TikTok called "CringeTok" has emerged as both symptom and response, with creators deliberately producing uncomfortable content.

**Why it is a signal:** Social inhibition is reshaping how young people behave in public spaces. The spontaneous, uninhibited social moments that beverages traditionally facilitate are becoming culturally fraught. This creates both challenge and opportunity for brands.

**Societal layer:** Identity / belonging / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Venues and products that create "safe spaces" for authentic expression—without recording, without judgment—may command premium positioning. NA beverages can position as enablers of genuine connection without the vulnerability of intoxication.

**What to monitor:** Growth of phone-free venues; emergence of "authentic expression" as a marketed experience; Gen Z attitudes toward public social behaviour.

### SIGNAL 4: Hospitality's Structural Cost Crisis

**What happened:** UK hospitality operating costs have risen 48% over a decade, with training costs up 70%. Only 25% of staff survive past 90 days, creating a cycle of investment loss and margin pressure.

**Why it is a signal:** This isn't cyclical—it's structural transformation. The hospitality model that existed pre-2015 is economically unviable. Survivors will be those who fundamentally reimagine operations, not those who optimise around the edges.

**Societal layer:** Control / community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Higher-margin NA beverages become strategically important for venue economics. Brands that help venues reduce complexity while maintaining experience quality will earn preferential placement.

**What to monitor:** Venue closure rates by category; staff retention innovations; emergence of new hospitality formats with different cost structures.

### SIGNAL 5: Altitude Drinking—Premium at 30,000 Feet

**What happened:** Qatar Airways partnered with Bar Leone's Lorenzo Antinori to create a 15-drink cocktail and non-alcoholic program specifically designed for altitude consumption, acknowledging that taste perception drops 30% at cruising altitude.

**Why it is a signal:** Premium hospitality is extending into transit spaces, and the science of consumption context is becoming a differentiator. This legitimises functional formulation as a premium attribute rather than a compromise.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages designed for specific consumption contexts—altitude, post-exercise, late-night—can command premium positioning. Context-specific formulation is an underexploited innovation space.

**What to monitor:** Airline beverage program evolution; emergence of context-specific beverage categories; consumer willingness to pay for optimised experiences.

### SIGNAL 6: The Body Optimisation Continuum

**What happened:** "Scrotox" emerged as the latest body-maxxing trend, while research showed weight-loss drugs could prevent thousands of knee replacements annually. The body is increasingly viewed as optimisable through intervention.

**Why it is a signal:** The medicalisation of appearance and function is normalising intervention across all body systems. What you consume is increasingly evaluated through an optimisation lens—not just "is this enjoyable?" but "what is this doing for me?"

**Societal layer:** Control / identity / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional claims must be specific and credible. Vague wellness positioning will lose to products with measurable, communicable benefits. The bar for "functional" is rising.

**What to monitor:** Consumer literacy around functional ingredients; regulatory evolution on health claims; emergence of personalised beverage recommendations based on biometrics.

### SIGNAL 7: Soil as Climate Resilience Narrative

**What happened:** Wine producers are increasingly positioning soil composition—particularly clay content—as the answer to climate change, with Italian geologist Carlo Ferretti arguing soil is "an active system that can either buffer or amplify climatic stress."

**Why it is a signal:** Terroir is being reframed from romantic marketing to functional climate adaptation. This creates a template for how beverage brands can discuss sustainability through specificity rather than generality.

**Societal layer:** Identity / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands can adopt similar specificity around ingredient sourcing and environmental resilience. "Where it comes from" becomes "how it survives"—a more compelling narrative for climate-conscious consumers.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to climate-resilience messaging; evolution of terroir narratives in non-wine categories; emergence of "climate-adapted" as a product attribute.

### SIGNAL 8: The Deposit Return Scheme Reality

**What happened:** UK Deposit Return Scheme handling fees were confirmed ahead of the October 2027 launch, with manual return points receiving 3p per container and RVM operators receiving tiered fees based on volume.

**Why it is a signal:** Circular economy infrastructure is becoming operational reality, not theoretical future. Brands must now factor return logistics into packaging decisions, and the economics favour certain formats over others.

**Societal layer:** Control / community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Packaging format decisions now carry operational cost implications. Brands should evaluate whether their packaging choices align with emerging return infrastructure economics.

**What to monitor:** Retailer adoption rates; consumer behaviour around returns; packaging format shifts in response to DRS economics.

### SIGNAL 9: White Wine's Asian Ascent

**What happened:** Vinexpo Hong Kong reported growing Asian appetite for white wines, particularly New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling, and Moscato, with 75% of attendees from Greater China.

**Why it is a signal:** Asian palate preferences are shifting toward lighter, fresher styles—a trend that aligns with NA beverage flavour profiles. This creates export opportunities for brands with appropriate flavour architectures.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages with crisp, aromatic profiles may find receptive markets in Asia. Flavour development should consider Asian palate preferences as a primary rather than secondary consideration.

**What to monitor:** Asian import data for light wine styles; NA beverage penetration in Asian markets; flavour preference research in key Asian markets.

### SIGNAL 10: The Palate Gap Relationship

**What happened:** Dazed explored "palate gap relationships"—couples with divergent food preferences—highlighting how dietary compatibility has become a relationship consideration worthy of cultural commentary.

**Why it is a signal:** Food and beverage preferences are increasingly viewed as identity markers that affect relationship compatibility. What you consume says something about who you are and who you're compatible with.

**Societal layer:** Identity / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages can position as "bridge" products that work across palate preferences, or as identity markers for specific consumer tribes. Understanding the social dynamics of shared consumption is strategically important.

**What to monitor:** Dating app integration of food/beverage preferences; social media discourse around consumption compatibility; emergence of "palate matching" as a concept.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Unrecorded Room

**Hypothesis:** Gen Z consumers will pay a premium for hospitality experiences in phone-free environments where they can express themselves without fear of being recorded, and NA beverages positioned as "social enablers" will outperform in these contexts.

**Method:** Partner with three venues in different markets to create designated phone-free spaces with Faraday cage technology or enforced phone storage. Stock exclusively with NA beverages positioned around authentic connection. Track dwell time, spend per head, repeat visits, and qualitative feedback on social comfort.

**Success metric:** 25% higher spend per head versus phone-permitted areas; 40% of visitors citing "freedom to be myself" in exit surveys; 30% repeat visit rate within 60 days.

---

### Experiment 2: Context-Optimised Formulation

**Hypothesis:** NA beverages formulated for specific consumption contexts (altitude, post-exercise, late-night) with scientifically-backed adjustments for taste perception changes will command 20%+ price premiums over general-purpose alternatives.

**Method:** Develop three SKUs optimised for specific contexts: one for airline service (enhanced citrus/umami for altitude taste suppression), one for gym/recovery (electrolyte-forward with reduced sweetness), one for late-night venues (caffeine-free with calming botanicals). Test in appropriate channels with A/B pricing against standard formulations.

**Success metric:** 20% price premium acceptance; 35% of purchasers correctly identifying the context-specific benefit unprompted; repeat purchase rate 15% higher than standard SKUs.

---

### Experiment 3: Future Heritage Documentation

**Hypothesis:** NA beverages with documented, traceable provenance—including batch numbers, ingredient sourcing stories, and production dates—will generate collector behaviour and secondary market value within 24 months.

**Method:** Create a limited release (500 bottles) with blockchain-verified provenance, detailed production documentation, and explicit "archive" positioning. Partner with a respected auction house to establish secondary market legitimacy. Track collector registration, secondary market transactions, and price appreciation.

**Success metric:** 100% sell-through within 30 days; minimum 50% price appreciation in secondary market within 12 months; establishment of "NA collectibles" as a recognised category in auction house communications.
