## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a fundamental tension between institutional scale and intimate authenticity—a push-pull dynamic that beverage brands must navigate with precision. The English wine industry's extraordinary 2025 vintage (55% production increase, 16.5 million bottles) signals that climate-driven terroir shifts are creating new legitimacy narratives for previously marginal producers. Simultaneously, we see the commodification of human connection reaching new forms: Russian men hiring strangers for barbecue companionship, the rise of "interoception" as a sixth sense governing emotional regulation, and the persistent stigma around male infertility finally entering mainstream discourse.

The World Cup is delivering a rare bright spot for on-trade beer sales (15.4% increase in host cities), but this masks deeper structural questions about how communal drinking occasions are evolving. Meanwhile, China's alcohol industry is calling for tighter e-commerce regulation—a signal that digital distribution has fundamentally disrupted traditional gatekeeping, creating both opportunity and chaos.

Three undercurrents demand attention: First, the body-as-project paradigm is being challenged by practices like Brazilian jiu-jitsu that prioritise function over appearance—a potential opening for beverages positioned around physical capability rather than aesthetic wellness. Second, the "smart people watching Love Island" phenomenon reveals how cultural consumption has become deliberately stratified, with educated consumers seeking permission structures for lowbrow pleasure. Third, the approval of IPE (inulin propionate ester) as a food additive that promotes satiety signals a future where functional ingredients become invisible infrastructure rather than marketing claims.

For non-alcoholic beverage strategists, the through-line is clear: authenticity now requires demonstrated craft heritage, functional benefits must address emotional regulation not just physical performance, and social occasions increasingly need facilitation rather than mere accompaniment.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Legitimacy Vintage

**What happened:** English wine production surged 55% in 2025 to 16.5 million bottles, with The Real Review's blind tasting placing all top 30 wines from England. Nyetimber won for the third time, while Coates & Seely vaulted from 12th to 3rd place.

**Why it is a signal:** Climate change is rewriting terroir hierarchies. Regions previously dismissed as marginal are now producing benchmark quality, creating new origin stories that challenge established prestige geographies. The "mast year" framing positions this as nature's endorsement rather than human ambition.

**Societal layer:** Status / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic producers should invest in terroir narratives now—botanical sourcing, water provenance, regional identity. The window for establishing "first mover" legitimacy in emerging NA origin stories is narrowing. English sparkling alternatives could leverage the same climate-quality narrative.

**What to monitor:** WineGB membership growth; NA brands citing specific UK botanical sourcing; hospitality venues creating "local" NA wine lists.

### SIGNAL 2: Rented Companionship Goes Mainstream

**What happened:** Russian men are hiring strangers for "barbecue companionship" at $13-65 per session—trained chefs and conversationalists who show up, grill meat, provide atmosphere, then leave with no expectation of ongoing friendship.

**Why it is a signal:** Loneliness has become so normalised that transactional companionship is now openly marketed without stigma. The service explicitly targets men, acknowledging gendered differences in social infrastructure. This isn't dating or therapy—it's purchasing the ambient presence of another human for a specific occasion.

**Societal layer:** Community / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Hospitality venues should consider "social facilitation" as a core service, not just food and drink provision. NA beverage brands could sponsor or create structured social occasions with built-in conversation frameworks—the drink becomes the excuse, but the real product is connection.

**What to monitor:** Growth of "rent-a-friend" services in Western markets; solo dining/drinking normalisation metrics; hospitality concepts explicitly designed for strangers to meet.

### SIGNAL 3: Interoception Enters the Wellness Lexicon

**What happened:** Research is establishing "interoception"—the body's ability to read its own internal signals—as a sixth sense with significant mental health implications. Studies link poor interoceptive awareness to mood swings, eating disorders, and anxiety.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a shift from external wellness metrics (steps, calories, sleep scores) to internal body literacy. The "hangry" phenomenon is being reframed as an interoceptive failure, not a personality quirk. This creates new language for discussing the mind-body connection.

**Societal layer:** Mental health / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages could be positioned around improving interoceptive awareness rather than delivering specific nutrients. "How does this make you feel?" becomes a legitimate product claim. Hospitality experiences could incorporate body-awareness moments—pauses, breathing, attention to sensation.

**What to monitor:** Interoception appearing in consumer wellness apps; meditation/mindfulness brands incorporating the term; functional food claims shifting from ingredients to sensations.

### SIGNAL 4: The Intelligent Lowbrow Permission Structure

**What happened:** Dazed explored why "the smartest person you know is watching Love Island," examining how educated consumers engage with reality TV through analytical frameworks that transform consumption into cultural criticism.

**Why it is a signal:** Cultural consumption is becoming deliberately stratified—people want permission to enjoy "lowbrow" pleasures while maintaining intellectual identity. The ironic distance isn't actually ironic anymore; it's a genuine mode of engagement that allows participation without perceived status loss.

**Societal layer:** Status / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands can lean into "guilty pleasure" positioning without apology. The sophisticated consumer doesn't need everything to be serious—they need permission structures. A non-alcoholic spritz positioned for "trash TV nights" could outperform one positioned for wellness occasions.

**What to monitor:** Meme culture around "smart people doing dumb things"; hospitality venues hosting viewing parties for reality TV; beverage brands using ironic/self-aware marketing.

### SIGNAL 5: The Body as Instrument, Not Project

**What happened:** Dazed profiled young women turning to Brazilian jiu-jitsu as a response to body dysmorphia, finding that the practice reframes the body as a functional instrument rather than an aesthetic project to be managed.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a counter-movement to the dominant "body as project" paradigm that drives much of wellness culture. The shift is from appearance-based to capability-based self-worth—what your body can do, not how it looks.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages positioned around physical capability (grip strength, endurance, recovery) rather than weight loss or beauty could tap this emerging identity. Sponsorship of functional fitness communities (climbing, martial arts, obstacle racing) over aesthetic fitness (yoga, pilates) may yield stronger brand affinity.

**What to monitor:** Growth of functional fitness modalities; decline in weight-loss-focused marketing effectiveness; athletic wear brands shifting messaging from appearance to performance.

### SIGNAL 6: Male Fertility Stigma Breaks

**What happened:** The Guardian podcast addressed "the lonely reality of male infertility," featuring a man who underwent years of IVF before discovering a treatable testicular issue. The piece explicitly names shame and guilt as barriers to diagnosis.

**Why it is a signal:** Male reproductive health is emerging from decades of silence. Sperm counts are declining globally, but the conversation has been almost entirely absent from mainstream discourse. This signals potential for a broader men's health reckoning.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Mental health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages targeting male reproductive health could find a receptive market as stigma decreases. More broadly, NA brands have an opportunity to position around "taking care of yourself" messaging that resonates with men who are beginning to engage with health beyond fitness.

**What to monitor:** Male fertility clinic marketing; supplement brands entering reproductive health; men's health media coverage shifting from fitness to broader wellness.

### SIGNAL 7: Invisible Functional Infrastructure

**What happened:** The EU approved IPE (inulin propionate ester), a powder that can be sprinkled into food to promote satiety. Unlike GLP-1 drugs, it's designed to prevent gradual weight gain rather than produce dramatic loss—and it's invisible in use.

**Why it is a signal:** Functional ingredients are moving from visible (supplements, shots, labelled products) to invisible (additives that disappear into regular food). This shifts the locus of control from consumer choice to food system design.

**Societal layer:** Control / Health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages could incorporate invisible functional ingredients that don't require marketing claims or consumer education. The product just "works" without the consumer needing to understand why. This challenges the current model of functional beverages that lead with ingredient stories.

**What to monitor:** Regulatory pathways for novel food ingredients; consumer research on "invisible" vs. "visible" functional benefits; food service adoption of functional additives.

### SIGNAL 8: Communal Drinking's World Cup Resurgence

**What happened:** US beer sales rose 5.5% during the World Cup, with host cities seeing 15.4% increases. Draught sales more than doubled in host markets. Even non-host states saw 1-3% beverage revenue increases.

**Why it is a signal:** Major sporting events remain one of the few reliable drivers of communal on-trade drinking. The data confirms that the occasion—not the beverage category—drives consumption. People gather first; they choose drinks second.

**Societal layer:** Community / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands must be present at communal sporting occasions with products that fit the social context—shareable formats, stadium-appropriate packaging, flavour profiles that work alongside (not instead of) beer. The goal is inclusion in the round, not replacement of it.

**What to monitor:** NA beverage presence at major sporting venues; watch party culture evolution; sports bar NA menu development.

### SIGNAL 9: China's E-Commerce Reckoning

**What happened:** China's alcohol industry association called for stronger regulation of online liquor sales, citing price distortions, high commission fees, and platform practices that pressure producers and distributors.

**Why it is a signal:** Digital distribution has disrupted traditional alcohol gatekeeping faster than regulation could adapt. The call for AI-powered consumer insights and blockchain traceability suggests the industry sees technology as both problem and solution.

**Societal layer:** Control / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands entering China should anticipate a regulatory environment that may favour traceability and authenticity verification. Building blockchain-verified supply chains now could become a competitive advantage. Platform dependency is a strategic risk.

**What to monitor:** Chinese e-commerce regulation developments; blockchain adoption in beverage supply chains; platform commission structures for NA categories.

### SIGNAL 10: The Mindful Masturbation Framework

**What happened:** Sexologists are promoting "mindful masturbation"—applying mindfulness principles to solo sex to develop body awareness that transfers to partnered experiences. The practice is framed as self-knowledge development, not just pleasure.

**Why it is a signal:** Sexual wellness is being reframed through the language of mindfulness and self-discovery. This represents the continued expansion of wellness frameworks into previously taboo domains, normalising intentionality around physical pleasure.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / Control / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** The broader signal is that intentionality is becoming valued across all pleasure domains. NA beverages positioned around "mindful drinking"—slowing down, savouring, being present—align with this cultural current. Evening/nighttime occasions could be reframed around intentional pleasure rather than wind-down utility.

**What to monitor:** Sexual wellness brand growth; mindfulness language appearing in pleasure-adjacent categories; consumer research on intentionality in consumption occasions.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Companion Drink

**Hypothesis:** Non-alcoholic beverages can serve as social facilitation tools for solo consumers seeking structured connection, similar to the "barbecue companion" model.

**Method:** Partner with a hospitality venue to create a monthly "Stranger's Table" event where solo attendees are seated together with a curated NA beverage pairing designed to prompt conversation (e.g., unusual flavour profiles that invite discussion, origin stories that provide talking points). The beverage becomes the shared reference point. Test against a control group with standard beverage service.

**Success metric:** Post-event surveys measuring perceived connection quality, likelihood to return, and spontaneous social media sharing. Target: 40% of attendees report making a meaningful new connection; 25% return for subsequent events.

---

### Experiment 2: Interoceptive Beverage Positioning

**Hypothesis:** Functional NA beverages positioned around "how you'll feel" rather than "what's in it" will outperform ingredient-led messaging with wellness-oriented consumers.

**Method:** Create two identical products with different marketing approaches. Version A leads with ingredients (adaptogens, nootropics, specific botanicals). Version B leads with sensations ("notice the calm," "feel the clarity," "sense the shift"). Deploy through the same channels to matched audience segments. Measure purchase intent, repeat purchase, and qualitative feedback on perceived efficacy.

**Success metric:** Version B achieves 20% higher repeat purchase rate and scores higher on "I could feel it working" in post-consumption surveys.

---

### Experiment 3: The Terroir Transfer

**Hypothesis:** English NA wine/sparkling alternatives can leverage the legitimacy gains of English wine by explicitly linking to the same climate-quality narrative and regional identity.

**Method:** Develop an NA sparkling product using English-grown botanicals and grapes (for flavour, not alcohol), with packaging and messaging that mirrors the aesthetic codes of premium English sparkling wine. Launch through the same hospitality channels that have embraced English wine (fine dining, boutique hotels, wedding venues). Track whether the "English provenance" story transfers credibility to the NA category.

**Success metric:** Achieve placement in 15+ venues that currently stock premium English sparkling wine within 6 months; qualitative research confirms consumers perceive the product as "legitimate" rather than "substitute."
