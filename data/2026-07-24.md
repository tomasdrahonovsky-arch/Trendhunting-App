## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural moment reveals a drinks industry in active recalibration—where quality infrastructure, premiumisation, and institutional legitimacy are converging to reshape what "serious drinking" means across categories. The most significant signal is Lucky Saint joining Cask Marque, marking the first time a non-alcoholic beer brand has entered the UK's premier beer quality accreditation system. This isn't merely symbolic; it represents the formalisation of no/low alcohol as a legitimate draught category requiring the same quality standards as traditional beer. The infrastructure of credibility is being extended to the sober-curious.

Simultaneously, we observe premiumisation accelerating in emerging markets. India's tariff reduction on Scotch whisky (150% to 75%) opens the world's largest whisky market to premium expressions, while Diageo's Indian subsidiary reports 52% profit growth driven almost entirely by premium spirits. The message: aspiration and quality are winning over volume plays globally.

In hospitality, the extension of experience beyond traditional service hours (BiBi Lates) signals a shift toward flexible, modular dining that accommodates contemporary social rhythms. Meanwhile, UK business rate cuts for pubs and live music venues suggest governmental recognition that community gathering spaces require structural support to survive.

The craft beer sector shows tentative signs of stabilisation in the US, with 54% of breweries reporting growth and taproom visits increasing. This suggests the category may be finding its floor after years of contraction.

For non-alcoholic beverage strategists, the critical insight is this: the infrastructure of legitimacy—accreditation, quality standards, premium positioning, and institutional recognition—is now available to categories previously considered peripheral. The question is no longer whether non-alcoholic drinks belong in serious drinking occasions, but how quickly brands can build the credibility architecture to claim that space.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: Non-Alcoholic Beer Enters Quality Accreditation Infrastructure

**What happened:** Lucky Saint became the first non-alcoholic beer brand to join Cask Marque as a corporate member, coinciding with the accreditation body's new "Perfect Pint Promise" standard that now includes all beer regardless of alcohol content.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents the formalisation of no/low alcohol as a legitimate draught category requiring identical quality infrastructure to traditional beer. Cask Marque's executive director explicitly acknowledged "great potential growth" in draught no/low, signalling institutional acceptance.

**Societal layer:** Status / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands should pursue third-party quality certifications aggressively. The credibility gap between alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks is closing through institutional mechanisms, not just marketing. Hospitality operators should expect—and demand—quality standards for NA draught.

**What to monitor:** Other accreditation bodies extending standards to NA categories; hospitality groups mandating NA quality certifications; line-cleaning protocols specifically for NA draught.

### SIGNAL 2: India's Premium Spirits Acceleration

**What happened:** The UK-India Free Trade Agreement halved Scotch whisky tariffs from 150% to 75%, while Diageo's Indian subsidiary reported 52% quarterly profit growth driven by premium brands accounting for 91.7% of net sales.

**Why it is a signal:** India—the world's largest whisky market—is shifting from volume to value. Premium brands are growing at 10%+ while the infrastructure for imported spirits improves. This represents a massive addressable market for premium positioning.

**Societal layer:** Status / aspiration

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium non-alcoholic spirits have an emerging opportunity in India's aspirational drinking culture. Brands should monitor Karnataka's new strength-based taxation as a model for how regulatory frameworks can favour premium products.

**What to monitor:** Indian state-level alcohol regulations; premium NA spirits distribution in Indian metros; Diageo's potential NA portfolio expansion in India.

### SIGNAL 3: Flexible Hospitality Formats Emerge

**What happened:** London's BiBi restaurant launched "BiBi Lates"—a post-9pm format offering à la carte dishes from the tasting menu alongside an expanded cocktail list, including non-alcoholic options like cold-brewed teas.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents hospitality's adaptation to contemporary social rhythms—modular experiences that don't require full commitment. The explicit inclusion of non-alcoholic options signals their integration into premium late-night occasions.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Late-night occasions remain under-served by sophisticated NA options. Brands should develop products specifically positioned for post-dinner, social-but-controlled drinking occasions. Cold-brewed teas and complex non-alcoholic cocktails have runway here.

**What to monitor:** Premium restaurant late-night format launches; NA beverage menu expansion in evening service; social media content around "late night sober" occasions.

### SIGNAL 4: UK Government Prioritises Community Hospitality

**What happened:** New Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced 20% business rate cuts for pubs, clubs, and live music venues—saving the typical pub £1,100 annually—as part of explicit policy to "save the hospitality sector."

**Why it is a signal:** Government recognition that community gathering spaces require structural intervention to survive. This positions pubs and venues as essential social infrastructure, not merely commercial enterprises.

**Societal layer:** Community / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Pubs positioned as community hubs will need inclusive beverage offerings. NA drinks become essential to serving the full community—designated drivers, pregnant women, health-conscious patrons, and the sober-curious. Brands should partner with pub groups on community-focused programming.

**What to monitor:** Pub group community programming initiatives; local authority support for hospitality; NA beverage ranging in community-focused venues.

### SIGNAL 5: Craft Beer Shows Stabilisation Signals

**What happened:** The Brewers Association's midyear survey found 54% of US craft breweries reporting growth (up from 49% last year), with taproom visits reaching their highest level since 2019-2020 at 5.5 visits per craft drinker annually.

**Why it is a signal:** After years of contraction, craft beer may be finding its floor. Critically, taprooms and regional breweries are leading growth—suggesting the experiential, community-oriented model is more resilient than distribution-focused approaches.

**Societal layer:** Community / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Craft NA beverages should prioritise taproom/direct-to-consumer models over broad distribution. The craft playbook—community, experience, authenticity—transfers directly to NA categories. Breweries expanding NA offerings should lead with taproom experiences.

**What to monitor:** Craft brewery NA product launches; taproom-exclusive NA releases; craft beer festival NA programming.

### SIGNAL 6: Champagne Production Caps Signal Category Discipline

**What happened:** Champagne set its 2026 marketable yield at 8,800kg/ha—the lowest in the modern era outside COVID—representing a collective decision to "gradually rebalance stock while preserving economic viability."

**Why it is a signal:** Premium categories are choosing scarcity over volume to protect value. This discipline-over-growth model represents a maturation strategy that prioritises long-term brand equity.

**Societal layer:** Status / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium NA brands should consider controlled distribution and limited releases rather than aggressive volume growth. Scarcity can build category credibility. The Champagne model of collective category management offers lessons for emerging NA categories.

**What to monitor:** Premium NA brand distribution strategies; limited release NA products; category-level coordination among NA producers.

### SIGNAL 7: Climate Adaptation Reshapes Wine Production

**What happened:** Domaine Lafage began its earliest-ever harvest (July 23) in Roussillon, driven by budburst 15 days early and extreme heat from May onwards. The estate has implemented regenerative viticulture practices including water management and agroforestry.

**Why it is a signal:** Climate adaptation is no longer theoretical—it's operational. Producers are fundamentally restructuring practices, and early harvests are becoming normalised. This affects flavour profiles, production timing, and sustainability narratives.

**Societal layer:** Control / spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Climate narratives are becoming central to premium beverage positioning. NA brands should develop clear sustainability stories and consider climate-adaptive sourcing. Regenerative agriculture certifications (like A Greener World) offer credibility infrastructure.

**What to monitor:** Harvest timing trends; regenerative agriculture certification adoption; climate-forward brand positioning in beverages.

### SIGNAL 9: Sexual Frequency Decoupled from Relationship Intensity

**What happened:** Research published in Current Sexual Health Reports found that being in love correlates with higher sexual frequency, but the intensity of love does not predict sexual frequency. Four distinct "lover categories" were identified with different behavioural patterns.

**Why it is a signal:** This challenges assumptions about intensity and behaviour correlation—relevant because beverage marketing often assumes emotional intensity drives consumption intensity. The research suggests behaviour is more categorical than linear.

**Societal layer:** Identity / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Consumer segmentation should focus on behavioural categories rather than intensity gradients. The "libidinous" category (highest openness, energy, desire to travel) suggests a distinct psychographic for adventurous NA beverage positioning.

**What to monitor:** Psychographic segmentation research; behavioural clustering in beverage consumption; occasion-based rather than intensity-based marketing.

**What happened:** The Guardian's year-long reporting project across 17 English and Welsh coastal towns revealed young people "left isolated" by 73% decline in youth services funding, yet remaining "resilient and full of hope."

**Why it is a signal:** Geographic inequality in opportunity is creating distinct youth subcultures in overlooked areas. These communities represent both underserved markets and potential sources of authentic cultural production.

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Coastal and peripheral communities represent underserved markets for youth-oriented NA beverages. Brands should consider place-based marketing and community investment in overlooked geographies rather than concentrating on metropolitan markets.

**What to monitor:** Youth culture emergence in non-metropolitan areas; coastal community programming; place-based brand partnerships.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: NA Draught Quality Certification Programme

**Hypothesis:** Hospitality venues displaying third-party NA quality certifications will see 25%+ increase in NA draught sales, as credibility signals reduce consumer hesitation.

**Method:** Partner with 20 UK pubs/bars to implement visible NA quality certification (building on Cask Marque's Perfect Pint Promise framework). Train staff on NA line-cleaning protocols. Install certification signage at point of sale. Track NA draught sales for 12 weeks versus matched control venues.

**Success metric:** 25% increase in NA draught volume sales; 15% increase in NA beverage mentions in customer reviews; staff confidence scores in NA recommendations.

---

### Experiment 2: Late-Night NA Cocktail Menu Development

**Hypothesis:** Premium restaurants offering dedicated post-9pm NA cocktail menus will capture an underserved occasion—sophisticated late-night socialising without alcohol—driving incremental revenue and attracting new customer segments.

**Method:** Develop a "Lates" NA cocktail menu with 6-8 complex, spirit-alternative drinks for 10 partner restaurants. Position explicitly for post-dinner occasions. Track orders by time of day, customer feedback, and repeat visits. Compare revenue per cover for late-night service with and without dedicated NA menu.

**Success metric:** NA beverages comprising 20%+ of post-9pm beverage orders; average check increase of 10% for late-night service; customer acquisition of previously non-visiting demographics.

---

### Experiment 3: Taproom-First NA Craft Launch

**Hypothesis:** Launching a premium NA craft beverage exclusively through taproom/direct channels (rather than broad distribution) will build stronger brand equity and command 30%+ price premium versus distributed competitors.

**Method:** Partner with 5 craft breweries to develop exclusive NA products available only at taproom and direct-to-consumer. Create taproom-specific experiences (tastings, pairings, brewer conversations). Measure willingness-to-pay, repeat purchase rates, and social media advocacy versus comparable distributed NA products.

**Success metric:** 30% price premium acceptance; 40% repeat purchase rate within 90 days; 3x social media mention rate versus distributed NA comparables.
