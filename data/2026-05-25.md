## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a society grappling with the erosion of consent, authenticity, and genuine connection across multiple domains. The most significant through-line is a growing tension between technological/commercial forces that extract value from people and a counter-movement seeking simplicity, presence, and honest engagement.

The tech consent crisis—where AI integration happens without user permission—mirrors the "future faking" epidemic in dating, where 92% of singles have experienced false promises of commitment. Both reflect a culture where consent and authenticity have become negotiable, creating widespread distrust. This distrust extends to health information, where perimenopause misinformation on social media is causing real harm, and to institutions, where NHS outsourcing and social care failures erode confidence in collective support systems.

Against this backdrop, we see a yearning for the genuine and simple. The Honey & Co. philosophy of "humble little treats" and "simple, delicious moments" speaks to a desire for uncomplicated pleasure. The finding that 90% of UK adults have positive memories of outdoor childhood play—yet half now spend less than three hours weekly in nature—reveals a profound disconnection from sources of authentic wellbeing.

The BuzzBallz 99p shot controversy is particularly relevant: alcohol brands are aggressively targeting younger demographics with cheap, TikTok-friendly products, creating a strategic opening for non-alcoholic alternatives that offer genuine value rather than exploitation.

For beverage and hospitality brands, this moment demands radical honesty, consent-forward practices, and experiences that deliver on their promises. The winners will be those who help people reconnect with simple pleasures, genuine community, and their own agency—without the manipulation that has become endemic to modern commercial life.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Consent Collapse

**What happened:** A Dazed article articulates growing frustration with AI integration across platforms, noting that users cannot opt out of AI features they never requested. The author describes feeling that technology adoption is happening "out of my control."

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a fundamental shift in the user-technology relationship from participation to subjugation. When people feel technology is imposed rather than chosen, it creates a market for alternatives that respect autonomy.

**Societal layer:** Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brands that offer transparent, opt-in experiences—where customers clearly understand what they're getting—will differentiate sharply. Consider "no algorithm" venues or products with radically simple ingredient lists and no hidden data collection.

**What to monitor:** Growth of "consent-forward" language in marketing; emergence of tech-free hospitality spaces; consumer backlash against AI-powered recommendation systems.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Future Faking and the Trust Deficit

**What happened:** Tawkify survey reveals 92% of singles have experienced "future faking"—romantic promises made without intention to follow through. 65% had never heard the term, suggesting the behaviour is normalised. Most relationships ended within one month of false promises.

**Why it is a signal:** This reflects a broader cultural pattern where commitment language has become performative rather than meaningful. The gap between stated intentions and actual behaviour is creating a generation sceptical of all promises.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Under-promise and over-deliver. Avoid aspirational marketing that can't be substantiated. Create experiences that build trust through consistency rather than grand claims. Consider "first date" beverage pairings or hospitality concepts designed for authentic early-stage connection.

**What to monitor:** Dating app feature evolution; growth of "slow dating" movements; consumer response to brands that make modest, verifiable claims.

---

### SIGNAL 3: The Humble Pleasure Renaissance

**What happened:** Honey & Co. positions their cookbook and café around "humble little treats" and "simple, delicious moments," explicitly rejecting complexity and ceremony in favour of accessible daily bliss.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a counter-movement to performative consumption and Instagram-worthy experiences. The emphasis on "clocking out" and finding "pure bliss" in ordinary moments suggests fatigue with optimisation culture.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Develop products and experiences positioned around everyday ritual rather than special occasions. The opportunity is in the 3pm pause, the morning commute, the quiet evening—not the celebration. Simplicity of ingredient and preparation becomes a feature, not a limitation.

**What to monitor:** Growth of "ordinary luxury" positioning; decline of "treat yourself" messaging; consumer interest in daily rituals versus occasional indulgences.

### SIGNAL 4: Predatory Pricing in Alcohol Targets Youth

**What happened:** Alcohol charities criticise BuzzBallz's new 99p shot, warning that cheap pricing and heavy TikTok marketing are designed to appeal to children while "hiding behind a thin 'nostalgia' label."

**Why it is a signal:** Alcohol brands are becoming more aggressive in targeting younger demographics through social media and price accessibility. This creates both a public health concern and a strategic opening for alternatives.

**Societal layer:** Rebellion / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Position non-alcoholic options as the sophisticated, self-aware choice—not the restrictive one. Young consumers rejecting exploitative marketing may embrace brands that treat them as intelligent adults. Price positioning matters: neither predatory-cheap nor exclusionary-expensive.

**What to monitor:** Regulatory responses to alcohol marketing on TikTok; Gen Z alcohol consumption data; growth of "sober curious" content creators.

### SIGNAL 5: Nature Deficit and Childhood Memory

**What happened:** Survey finds 90% of UK adults have positive memories of outdoor childhood play, yet 50% now spend less than three hours weekly in nature. Wildlife charities urge people to "rewild their inner child."

**Why it is a signal:** There's a profound gap between what people know makes them happy (nature, freedom, sensory engagement) and how they actually live. This nostalgia is actionable—people want to return to these states but lack pathways.

**Societal layer:** Spirituality / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Create products and experiences that evoke outdoor, sensory, childhood-adjacent feelings. Botanical ingredients with foraging narratives; venues with natural elements; packaging that references natural textures. The opportunity is in helping adults access states they remember but can't reach.

**What to monitor:** Growth of forest bathing and outdoor wellness; "rewilding" as lifestyle concept; success of nature-adjacent hospitality concepts.

### SIGNAL 6: Health Misinformation and the Expertise Vacuum

**What happened:** Multiple Guardian articles highlight perimenopause misinformation on social media causing real harm—unintended pregnancies, unnecessary medication, missed diagnoses. Experts struggle to counter viral false claims.

**Why it is a signal:** Institutional health expertise is losing ground to social media content. People are making significant health decisions based on unverified information, creating both risk and opportunity for trusted voices.

**Societal layer:** Mental Health / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverage claims must be rigorously substantiated—the backlash against health misinformation will eventually reach wellness products. Brands that partner with credible experts and avoid overclaiming will build durable trust. Consider menopause-specific functional beverages developed with clinical input.

**What to monitor:** Regulatory scrutiny of functional beverage claims; consumer demand for clinical validation; growth of expert-led wellness content.

### SIGNAL 7: Youth Employment Crisis and Identity Formation

**What happened:** UK government announces 300,000 extra work experience placements after warnings that Britain spends £25 keeping young people on benefits for every £1 helping them into work. Minister describes a "quiet crisis" in youth employment.

**Why it is a signal:** A generation is struggling to form adult identities through traditional work pathways. This affects consumption patterns, social venues, and the role of third spaces in young adult life.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Young adults without traditional work structures need alternative spaces for identity formation and social connection. Affordable third spaces that offer belonging without employment-based status markers. Consider hospitality concepts that integrate skill-building or community contribution.

**What to monitor:** Evolution of co-working spaces; growth of community-oriented hospitality; young adult spending patterns on experiences versus goods.

### SIGNAL 8: Institutional Care Failure

**What happened:** Social care described as "worse than ever" despite government promises. NHS outsourcing diagnostic scan analysis to private firms at record costs due to staffing failures.

**Why it is a signal:** Trust in institutional care is eroding, pushing people toward self-care, community care, and commercial wellness alternatives. This creates both opportunity and responsibility for brands entering the wellness space.

**Societal layer:** Community / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Wellness-positioned beverages and hospitality experiences are filling gaps left by institutional care failures. This demands ethical responsibility—brands must not exploit vulnerability. Opportunity in community-building hospitality that provides genuine support networks.

**What to monitor:** Growth of peer support communities; consumer spending on preventive wellness; regulatory attention to wellness claims.

### SIGNAL 9: The Authenticity Paradox in Entertainment

**What happened:** Family Guy voice actor refused to record a joke about Jesus he found gratuitously offensive, questioning why diminishing returns of shock value were worth alienating audiences.

**Why it is a signal:** Even within entertainment industries built on provocation, there's recognition that authenticity and genuine creative value matter more than shock. Audiences are developing more sophisticated filters for what deserves attention.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Edgy or provocative brand positioning must deliver genuine value, not just attention. The "shock for shock's sake" approach is losing effectiveness. Authenticity means having actual values and occasionally saying no.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to brand provocations; growth of "values-led" brand positioning; backlash against performative edginess.

### SIGNAL 10: Gaming Community Mobilisation

**What happened:** Destiny 3 petition reaches 100K signatures in two days as fans ask Sony to save the franchise after Bungie announces the end of Destiny 2 with no sequel planned.

**Why it is a signal:** Gaming communities can mobilise rapidly around shared identity and belonging. The intensity of response to a game's end reveals how deeply these communities matter to participants' sense of self.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Gaming communities represent underserved markets for social beverages and hospitality. These are highly engaged, identity-driven consumers who gather both online and increasingly offline. Consider gaming-adjacent venue concepts or beverages positioned for extended play sessions.

**What to monitor:** Growth of gaming cafés and bars; esports viewership demographics; gaming community crossover with other lifestyle categories.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Consent-Forward Café

**Hypothesis:** Consumers fatigued by non-consensual technology integration will pay a premium for hospitality experiences that explicitly respect their autonomy and privacy.

**Method:** Launch a pop-up café concept with explicit "consent principles": no data collection, no loyalty apps required, no AI recommendations, clear ingredient sourcing, staff trained to ask permission before making suggestions. Market as "a space that asks before it acts." Offer a curated non-alcoholic beverage menu with transparent sourcing. Test in a tech-heavy urban market (e.g., Shoreditch, San Francisco).

**Success metric:** 40% of customers cite "consent principles" as reason for visit in exit surveys; 25% price premium acceptance versus comparable venues; social media sentiment analysis showing "trust" and "respect" as dominant themes.

---

### Experiment 2: The Humble Daily Ritual Subscription

**Hypothesis:** Consumers seeking simple daily pleasures will subscribe to a non-alcoholic beverage service positioned around ordinary moments rather than special occasions.

**Method:** Develop a subscription service delivering one "humble pleasure" beverage daily—positioned explicitly for the 3pm slump, the morning pause, or the evening wind-down. Packaging emphasises simplicity and routine. Marketing avoids aspiration; focuses on "the small moment that's actually yours." Include a physical ritual card with each delivery suggesting a 2-minute pause practice. Test with 500 subscribers over 8 weeks.

**Success metric:** 70% retention after 8 weeks; qualitative feedback emphasising "ritual" and "pause" language; 50% of subscribers report improved daily wellbeing in exit survey.

---

### Experiment 3: The Rewilding Beverage Experience

**Hypothesis:** Adults who report positive childhood outdoor memories but limited current nature exposure will respond strongly to beverages and experiences that evoke sensory outdoor states.

**Method:** Create a limited-edition non-alcoholic beverage line with three variants tied to specific outdoor childhood memories: "Muddy Puddle" (earthy, grounding), "Hedgerow" (wild berry, foraging), "Tree Climb" (bark, resin, height). Partner with a nature charity for authenticity. Host tasting events in urban parks with guided "micro-rewilding" activities. Measure emotional response and purchase intent.

**Success metric:** 60% of tasters report "nostalgia" or "childhood memory" in unprompted feedback; 35% conversion to purchase; 20% sign up for nature charity newsletter (indicating authentic connection to positioning).
