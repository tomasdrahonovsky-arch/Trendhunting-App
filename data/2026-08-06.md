## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound recalibration of how consumers seek authenticity, belonging, and meaning—particularly through experiences that transcend traditional consumption patterns. The fine wine industry's structural crisis, with UK merchants collectively losing £20 million annually, signals not merely economic headwinds but a generational shift away from accumulation-based status markers. Meanwhile, Bordeaux's revival of "claret" as a chillable, accessible category demonstrates legacy institutions scrambling to meet consumers where they actually are: seeking lighter, more casual, and less intimidating entry points.

The camping and outdoor experience economy emerges as fertile territory for brands willing to embed authentically rather than merely advertise seasonally. This connects to a broader pattern: consumers increasingly value context-specific rituals over product-centric consumption. The experience must feel native to the moment, not imported.

China's "Guochao" movement—where serving Chinese wine over French at banquets is now considered "cool"—represents a significant cultural confidence shift with implications far beyond wine. Heritage and provenance are being reinterpreted through local rather than colonial lenses.

Health narratives continue evolving. Global alcohol-attributable deaths falling 26% since 2010 provides ammunition for moderation messaging, while NHS warnings about summer heat crises signal how climate anxiety increasingly shapes wellness conversations. The intersection of health consciousness and environmental awareness creates new permission structures for functional, non-alcoholic alternatives.

For non-alcoholic beverage strategists, the through-line is clear: consumers seek products that facilitate belonging without requiring expertise, that honour local identity without parochialism, and that fit naturally into emerging rituals—whether around a campfire, during a heatwave, or at a banquet where cultural pride matters more than imported prestige.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Outdoor Ritual Economy

**What happened:** ThunderDonk whisky launched Campfire Coffee specifically targeting campers, with marketing experts noting camping as "incredibly fertile ground" for brand storytelling. Industry voices emphasise that outdoor positioning must be foundational, not seasonal.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a shift from occasion-based marketing to identity-based brand building. Consumers increasingly define themselves through experiential contexts (camping, festivals, outdoor gatherings) rather than product categories.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands have an underexploited opportunity in outdoor contexts where hydration, functionality, and social ritual intersect. Campfire-ready formats, portable packaging, and flavour profiles designed for outdoor consumption (smoky, warming, shareable) could capture this space before alcohol brands fully colonise it.

**What to monitor:** Outdoor recreation participation rates; festival beverage sales data; camping equipment retailers' beverage partnerships; #vanlife and outdoor lifestyle content creators' beverage choices.

### SIGNAL 2: Heritage Confidence Over Colonial Prestige

**What happened:** China's "Guochao" (China Chic) movement has shifted fine wine culture—serving Chinese wine over French at banquets is now considered sophisticated. LVMH is planting vineyards in Yunnan, and Ningxia estates are winning international competitions.

**Why it is a signal:** This marks the end of Western luxury's automatic cultural authority. Consumers in emerging markets increasingly prefer products that celebrate local heritage rather than importing foreign prestige markers.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands entering Asian markets should consider local ingredient stories and heritage narratives rather than defaulting to European sophistication codes. Functional ingredients with regional provenance (Chinese botanicals, Japanese fermentation traditions) may outperform imported "premium" positioning.

**What to monitor:** Luxury consumption patterns in China, India, and Southeast Asia; local craft beverage movements; social media sentiment around "buying local" in premium categories.

### SIGNAL 3: Fine Wine's Structural Reckoning

**What happened:** Cru World Wine's CEO stated the top 15 UK fine wine companies are losing nearly £20 million annually, with "too many sub-scale merchants and fragmented inventory holders." He suggested the generational appetite for building large wine collections may not continue.

**Why it is a signal:** This indicates a fundamental shift from accumulation-based luxury (collecting) to experience-based luxury (consuming). Younger consumers may drink quality but won't build cellars.

**Societal layer:** Status / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium non-alcoholic beverages should emphasise immediate experience quality over collectibility or aging potential. The "drink now, share now" mentality favours products designed for social moments rather than investment.

**What to monitor:** Wine storage facility occupancy rates; auction house sales demographics; subscription model adoption in premium beverages; social media sharing of "drinking" versus "collecting" content.

### SIGNAL 4: The Lightness Imperative

**What happened:** Bordeaux is officially reviving "claret" as a regulated category for light, low-tannin, unoaked, chillable red wines targeting younger consumers. The specification requires short maceration and fruit-forward profiles designed for immediate drinking.

**Why it is a signal:** The world's most prestigious red wine region is formally acknowledging that traditional full-bodied styles are losing relevance with younger drinkers. Lightness, accessibility, and chill-ability are now strategic priorities, not compromises.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** This validates the broader consumer preference for lighter, more refreshing beverages across all categories. Non-alcoholic options positioned as "sessionable," lower in sugar, and designed for warm-weather consumption align with this macro shift.

**What to monitor:** Chilled red wine sales; low-ABV and session-strength product launches; refrigeration merchandising in wine retail; summer beverage consumption patterns.

### SIGNAL 5: Climate-Driven Health Infrastructure

**What happened:** UK Health Secretary warned the NHS must prepare for annual summer crises from extreme heat, with A&E pressures matching winter flu season. Major infrastructure investment is planned for heat resilience.

**Why it is a signal:** Climate anxiety is becoming institutionalised in health policy. Summer is no longer the "easy" season for health systems, creating new wellness narratives around heat, hydration, and cooling.

**Societal layer:** Mental Health / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional hydration and cooling beverages gain legitimacy as health necessities, not merely lifestyle choices. Partnerships with health systems, workplace wellness programmes, and public health campaigns become viable distribution and credibility channels.

**What to monitor:** Heatwave-related hospitalisations; workplace hydration policies; public health messaging around summer wellness; electrolyte and functional hydration product sales during heat events.

### SIGNAL 6: The Harm Reduction Narrative Gains Data

**What happened:** IARD analysis found global alcohol-attributable deaths fell 26% since 2010, with disability-adjusted life years down 23%. This suggests meaningful progress toward WHO harm reduction targets.

**Why it is a signal:** Positive harm reduction data provides new framing opportunities. Rather than positioning non-alcoholic beverages against alcohol, brands can align with a broader cultural shift toward moderation that's already demonstrably underway.

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands can position themselves as part of a successful global health movement rather than as niche alternatives for non-drinkers. "Join the shift" messaging may resonate more than "replace your vice."

**Societal layer:** Control / Identity

**What to monitor:** WHO and public health messaging evolution; insurance industry wellness incentive programmes; corporate alcohol policies; moderation-focused social media communities.

### SIGNAL 10: Illicit Alcohol Crackdowns Intensify

**What happened:** Rwanda closed 101 alcohol production facilities and launched "Tunywe Less" (Let's Drink Less) campaign amid rising consumption, particularly among youth. Vietnam prosecuted a woman for selling spirits mixed with industrial alcohol and tap water.

**Why it is a signal:** Governments in developing markets are simultaneously cracking down on illicit production and promoting moderation messaging. This creates regulatory and cultural openings for legitimate, safer alternatives.

**Societal layer:** Control / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Markets with active illicit alcohol problems represent opportunities for non-alcoholic brands positioned around safety, legitimacy, and social responsibility. Government partnerships and public health alignment may accelerate market entry.

**What to monitor:** Illicit alcohol enforcement actions globally; public health campaign launches; youth drinking rate data; regulatory frameworks for non-alcoholic beverages in developing markets.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: Campfire-Ready Non-Alcoholic Ritual Kit

**Hypothesis:** Outdoor enthusiasts will adopt non-alcoholic beverages as campfire ritual anchors if products are designed specifically for the context—portable, warming, shareable, and narratively connected to outdoor identity.

**Method:** Develop a limited-edition "campfire kit" containing a concentrated non-alcoholic botanical blend designed to be heated over fire or camp stove, packaged in lightweight, recyclable materials with clear outdoor-specific usage instructions. Partner with 3-5 outdoor lifestyle content creators for authentic field testing and documentation. Distribute through outdoor equipment retailers (REI, Cotswold Outdoor) rather than traditional beverage channels.

**Success metric:** Kit sell-through rate above 60% within 8 weeks; user-generated content featuring product in outdoor settings exceeding 500 posts; repeat purchase intent above 40% in post-purchase survey.

---

### Experiment 2: Heritage Ingredient Provenance Platform

**Hypothesis:** Premium non-alcoholic beverages featuring locally-sourced, heritage ingredients with documented provenance stories will outperform imported "sophisticated" positioning in markets experiencing cultural confidence shifts (China, India, Middle East).

**Method:** Create three regional SKUs using ingredients with strong local heritage narratives (e.g., Chinese hawthorn, Indian kokum, Middle Eastern sumac). Develop QR-linked provenance documentation showing ingredient sourcing, farmer partnerships, and cultural significance. Test in premium hospitality venues in Shanghai, Mumbai, and Dubai with A/B comparison against existing "European-style" premium non-alcoholic options.

**Success metric:** Heritage SKUs achieve 25% higher reorder rates from venue partners; consumer willingness-to-pay premium of 15%+ over comparison products; social media sharing rate 2x higher for heritage variants.

---

### Experiment 3: Heatwave Hydration Partnership Pilot

**Hypothesis:** Positioning functional non-alcoholic beverages as climate-health necessities through public health partnerships will generate both distribution access and credibility that traditional marketing cannot achieve.

**Method:** Partner with one NHS Trust or local authority public health team to include branded functional hydration products in heatwave response protocols (cooling centres, vulnerable population outreach, workplace guidance). Provide products at cost or below, with co-branded educational materials on heat-related health risks. Document health outcomes and satisfaction data.

**Success metric:** Partnership renewal for subsequent heat events; measurable media coverage positioning brand within public health context; 20%+ increase in brand trust scores among target demographics in partnership region versus control region.
