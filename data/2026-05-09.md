## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between institutional authority and individual meaning-making. Across multiple domains—from drinks education to health crises to relationship structures—we observe consumers and communities actively rejecting prescribed frameworks in favour of personal interpretation and self-directed experience.

The WSET rebrand away from its classical Ariadne logo signals a broader democratisation of expertise, while Laphroaig's Willem Dafoe collaboration explicitly invites consumers to reject technical tasting notes in favour of subjective, creative interpretation. This mirrors the broader cultural moment where authority is questioned, expertise is flattened, and personal narrative trumps institutional validation.

Simultaneously, we see the consequences of institutional withdrawal. The hantavirus outbreak on MV Hondius exposes the fragility of global health systems post-US WHO withdrawal, creating anxiety that will reshape how consumers evaluate risk, travel, and communal experiences. This health scare arrives as cannabis addiction gains mainstream recognition—a correction to decades of "harmless" positioning that signals growing sophistication around substance relationships.

The emotional architecture of modern relationships is also shifting. Research into why situationship endings hurt more than formal breakups reveals a generation grappling with ambiguity as the default state—a pattern that extends beyond romance into how people relate to brands, communities, and commitments.

For non-alcoholic beverage brands, this moment demands careful positioning. Consumers want permission to interpret, not instruction. They seek community without rigid belonging requirements. They're increasingly aware of their relationship with substances—including caffeine, sugar, and adaptogens—and want honest dialogue rather than wellness theatre. The brands that thrive will be those that hold space for ambiguity while providing genuine value, not those that claim false certainty in uncertain times.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Death of Prescribed Meaning

**What happened:** Laphroaig launched "Willem by Willem" with Willem Dafoe, explicitly positioning the whisky around subjective interpretation rather than technical tasting notes. Dafoe stated he chose based on what made his "curiosity come alive" and invited consumers to "tell me" what they experience.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a fundamental shift from brand-as-authority to brand-as-facilitator. The accompanying creative competition (poems, sketches, films) positions consumption as co-creation rather than passive reception.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands should resist over-explaining functional benefits or flavour profiles. Create frameworks for personal interpretation—tasting journals, community sharing platforms, or "what does this make you feel?" prompts rather than "this contains X mg of Y."

**What to monitor:** Consumer-generated content around beverage experiences; decline in engagement with technical/educational content; rise of "vibe-based" product descriptions.

### SIGNAL 2: Institutional Expertise Seeks Accessibility

**What happened:** WSET is rebranding from "Wine & Spirit Education Trust" to simply "WSET: Global Drinks Education," removing its classical Ariadne logo depicting a figure holding a wine glass. CEO Michelle Brampton acknowledged the imagery "could feel exclusive or overly wine-focused."

**Why it is a signal:** The world's leading drinks education body is actively dismantling markers of exclusivity and tradition. This reflects broader pressure on gatekeeping institutions to democratise access while maintaining credibility.

**Societal layer:** Status / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Educational content around NA beverages should avoid replicating wine/spirits' historical gatekeeping. Position learning as accessible exploration, not credential acquisition. Partner with WSET on NA-specific programming as they expand beyond wine.

**What to monitor:** WSET curriculum additions; emergence of NA-specific certification programmes; hospitality staff training requirements.

### SIGNAL 3: Climate Anxiety Meets Plate Anxiety

**What happened:** Research published in Global Change Biology shows rising CO2 levels have reduced protein, iron, and zinc in staple crops by 3.2% since the late 1980s. Plants grow larger but are nutritionally diluted.

**Why it is a signal:** This transforms climate change from abstract environmental concern to immediate personal health threat. The "invisible" nature of nutrient depletion—food looks and tastes the same—creates a new category of anxiety around what we consume.

**Societal layer:** Control / Mental Health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages with verifiable, bioavailable nutrients gain relevance. However, brands must navigate between genuine value and exploiting anxiety. Transparency about sourcing, soil health, and nutrient density becomes competitive advantage.

**What to monitor:** Consumer interest in regenerative agriculture claims; demand for third-party nutrient verification; growth in supplement-beverage hybrids.

### SIGNAL 4: The Situationship Economy

**What happened:** Research and cultural commentary explain why situationship endings hurt more than formal relationship breakups—grieving "what could have been" rather than what was, combined with the addictive unpredictability of undefined connections.

**Why it is a signal:** Ambiguity has become the default relational state for younger consumers. This extends beyond romance to how people relate to brands, communities, and commitments—preferring optionality over definition.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Subscription models and loyalty programmes must accommodate commitment-averse consumers. Create value in the undefined space—"try us when you want" rather than "commit to us." Hospitality venues should design for fluid social configurations, not just couples or groups.

**What to monitor:** Subscription churn rates by age cohort; success of "no commitment" membership models; social media discourse around brand loyalty.

### SIGNAL 5: Cannabis Addiction Enters Mainstream Discourse

**What happened:** The Guardian published a substantial feature on cannabis addiction and dependency, including vivid accounts of compulsive behaviour (retrieving discarded vape cartridges from dumpsters) and acknowledging "misconceptions about the addictiveness of cannabis."

**Why it is a signal:** After decades of positioning cannabis as harmless—accelerated by legalisation movements—mainstream media is now platforming addiction narratives. This signals a maturation in how society discusses substance relationships.

**Societal layer:** Control / Mental Health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages positioned as "healthier alternatives" must avoid the trap of claiming harmlessness. Consumers are developing sophisticated frameworks for evaluating their relationships with all substances, including caffeine and sugar. Honest dialogue about consumption patterns builds trust.

**What to monitor:** Regulatory attention to functional ingredients (adaptogens, nootropics); consumer discourse around "healthy" substance dependencies; emergence of "mindful consumption" movements beyond alcohol.

**What happened:** Perrier-Jouët detailed its regenerative viticulture programme, noting Champagne yields are down 25% over 15 years. The house has 50% of vineyards under regenerative practices, targeting 100% by 2030, with focus on soil health, biodiversity, and climate resilience.

**Why it is a signal:** Sustainability is transitioning from marketing narrative to operational necessity. The 25% yield decline makes this existential, not aspirational. Regenerative practices are being framed as business continuity, not virtue signalling.

**Societal layer:** Control / Spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Ingredient sourcing stories must evolve from "organic" checkboxes to genuine regenerative narratives. Consumers increasingly understand that sustainable practices affect product availability and quality. Transparency about supply chain challenges builds credibility.

**What to monitor:** Regenerative certification adoption; ingredient price volatility communications; consumer willingness to pay premiums for verified regenerative sourcing.

### SIGNAL 10: Lighter Styles for Lifestyle Contexts

**What happened:** Southeast Asian island markets (Bali, Phuket, Koh Samui) are driving rosé growth, with heat and beach lifestyle pushing consumers toward lighter, lower-alcohol, refreshing options. Champagne leads, with rosé as close second.

**Why it is a signal:** Context is overriding traditional preferences. The same consumers who drink red wine in Bangkok choose rosé in Phuket. This confirms that occasion and environment are primary drivers, not fixed taste preferences.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages must be designed for specific contexts, not just general "health" positioning. Beach/resort NA options should prioritise refreshment, visual appeal, and social shareability. Partner with destination hospitality to become the contextually appropriate choice.

**What to monitor:** Resort and beach venue NA adoption; context-specific consumption research; visual/aesthetic trends in beverage presentation.

### SIGNAL 11: Dreaming While Awake—Blurred Consciousness States

**What happened:** Paris Brain Institute research found that dream-like, "bizarre" mental experiences occur during verified wakefulness, challenging the binary distinction between sleeping and waking consciousness.

**Why it is a signal:** Scientific validation of liminal consciousness states aligns with growing consumer interest in altered states, meditation, and psychedelic-adjacent experiences. The boundary between "normal" and "altered" consciousness is being questioned.

**Societal layer:** Spirituality / Mental Health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages targeting relaxation, focus, or mood should acknowledge the complexity of consciousness states. Avoid binary claims ("wake up" vs "wind down"). Create products for liminal moments—the transition spaces between states.

**What to monitor:** Consumer interest in nootropics and adaptogens; meditation and breathwork participation; language around "altered states" in wellness discourse.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Interpretation Bar

**Hypothesis:** Consumers will engage more deeply with NA beverages when invited to create personal meaning rather than receive prescribed flavour narratives.

**Method:** Partner with a hospitality venue to create a "no tasting notes" NA beverage menu. Each drink is presented with only a name and an invitation: "What does this make you feel?" Provide small cards for guests to write their interpretations, displayed anonymously on a communal board. Track dwell time, repeat orders, social sharing, and qualitative feedback comparing this format to traditional descriptive menus.

**Success metric:** 25% increase in average dwell time; 40% of guests contribute interpretations; 15% increase in social media mentions versus control period with traditional menu.

---

### Experiment 2: The Situationship Subscription

**Hypothesis:** Commitment-averse younger consumers will engage more with NA beverage brands that explicitly accommodate ambivalence rather than demanding loyalty.

**Method:** Launch a "No Strings" subscription model: no minimum commitment, pause anytime, skip without penalty, cancel via text. Marketing explicitly acknowledges that "we're not asking you to commit—just to try us when you want us." Compare acquisition, retention, and lifetime value against traditional subscription models. Survey subscribers on emotional response to messaging.

**Success metric:** 30% higher trial conversion than traditional subscription; comparable 6-month retention despite no commitment requirements; positive sentiment in qualitative feedback around "no pressure" positioning.

---

### Experiment 3: The Regenerative Transparency Dashboard

**Hypothesis:** Consumers will pay a premium for NA beverages that provide verifiable, real-time transparency about ingredient sourcing and regenerative practices.

**Method:** Develop a QR-code-linked dashboard for one product line showing: farm/source location, soil health metrics, harvest date, carbon footprint calculation, and farmer profiles. A/B test pricing at standard versus 15% premium with dashboard access. Track scan rates, time spent on dashboard, social sharing of sourcing stories, and purchase conversion at each price point.

**Success metric:** 60% of purchasers scan QR code; 20% of scanners share sourcing content; premium-priced variant achieves 70% of standard variant sales volume (indicating willingness to pay for transparency).
