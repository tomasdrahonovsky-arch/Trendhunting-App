## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a generation grappling with the tension between authenticity and performance, between institutional failure and personal agency. The dominant thread is a recalibration of what "wellness" actually means—moving beyond surface-level self-care into deeper questions of emotional honesty, sustained mental health recovery, and the courage to be genuinely seen.

Three forces converge: First, a crisis of institutional trust among young people who feel "the game is rigged," creating demand for brands and spaces that demonstrate genuine investment rather than performative concern. Second, a growing sophistication around mental health that rejects quick fixes—Clarke Carlisle's insistence that stability "doesn't happen by accident" and requires ongoing work mirrors a broader cultural shift toward maintenance over miracle cures. Third, a surprising return to analogue social dynamics, evidenced by the "SABSing" trend on college campuses where students deliberately make themselves visible in physical space rather than relying on algorithmic matchmaking.

The beverage and hospitality implications are significant. Consumers increasingly seek products and experiences that support sustained wellbeing rather than momentary escape. They want spaces that facilitate genuine human connection without the mediation of screens. And they're drawn to brands that acknowledge complexity—that understand recovery, identity, and belonging are ongoing processes rather than destinations.

The cultural moment demands honesty over aspiration. The most resonant brands will be those that meet people where they actually are: tired of performance, hungry for substance, and seeking communities that don't require them to pretend. For non-alcoholic beverages specifically, this creates opportunity to position as tools for presence and connection rather than substitutes for something else.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Maintenance Economy

**What happened:** Former footballer Clarke Carlisle, incoming chair of Mind, articulated that mental health recovery requires continuous work—"once you get stable that's not the end of the journey"—criticising society's "patch you up and send you on your way" approach.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a shift from crisis intervention to sustained maintenance as the framework for wellbeing. Consumers are rejecting the notion that health is a problem to be solved rather than a practice to be maintained.

**Societal layer:** Mental health / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Position products as daily rituals supporting ongoing wellness rather than occasional treats or crisis interventions. Subscription models and "maintenance" messaging could resonate strongly.

**What to monitor:** Language shifts in wellness marketing; growth of routine-based health products; consumer fatigue with "transformation" narratives.

---

### SIGNAL 2: The Return to Physical Visibility

**What happened:** "SABSing" (See And Be Seen) is emerging on college campuses—students deliberately positioning themselves in physical spaces to attract romantic attention, explicitly rejecting dating app culture.

**Why it is a signal:** Gen Z is actively creating alternatives to algorithmic social connection, seeking the spontaneity and embodied presence that digital platforms cannot replicate. This is rebellion against mediated experience.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Design venues and experiences that facilitate being seen—good lighting, comfortable lingering spaces, reasons to stay. Beverages become props for presence rather than consumption.

**What to monitor:** Campus social behaviours; decline in dating app engagement among younger cohorts; growth of "third place" venues near universities.

---

### SIGNAL 3: Emotional Fluency as Dating Currency

**What happened:** Dazed published guidance on dating when "afraid to say how you really feel," framing emotional expression as a skill to be developed rather than a personality trait.

**Why it is a signal:** Emotional honesty is being reframed as learnable competency rather than innate quality. This creates permission structures for vulnerability and signals demand for spaces that support authentic expression.

**Societal layer:** Identity / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Create environments and occasions that lower barriers to emotional honesty—intimate seating, conversation prompts, beverages positioned as "courage without alcohol."

**What to monitor:** Growth of communication coaching; dating content focused on emotional skills; demand for facilitated social experiences.

---

### SIGNAL 4: The Legitimisation of Strange Experience

**What happened:** A PLOS One study of 5,117 people found over 60% reported "nonordinary experiences" (déjà vu, sensing presences, visions) while awake and alert, with nearly half describing them neutrally or positively.

**Why it is a signal:** The boundary between "normal" and "paranormal" experience is softening. Consumers are increasingly comfortable discussing liminal states without pathologising them, creating space for products that acknowledge altered consciousness.

**Societal layer:** Spirituality / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages targeting altered states (calm, focus, presence) can lean into experiential language without fear. "Nonordinary" is becoming ordinary.

**What to monitor:** Mainstream media coverage of consciousness research; growth of meditation and breathwork communities; language around functional ingredients.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Generational Betrayal Narrative

**What happened:** Analysis of young people's attitudes revealed only one in four believe "everyone in society has a fair crack," with most feeling the system is rigged against them following austerity and pandemic.

**Why it is a signal:** This isn't cynicism—it's a coherent worldview that shapes consumption. Brands perceived as part of "the system" face scepticism; those demonstrating genuine investment in young people's futures earn trust.

**Societal layer:** Control / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Transparent pricing, genuine sustainability commitments, and visible investment in communities matter more than aspirational marketing. Authenticity audits are constant.

**What to monitor:** Brand trust metrics among under-30s; success of transparent pricing models; backlash against perceived "greenwashing."

---

### SIGNAL 6: The Cellular Unification Theory

**What happened:** The Guardian explored emerging neuroscience suggesting sleep, exercise, and diet all work through similar cellular mechanisms—they're "essentially doing the same job."

**Why it is a signal:** Holistic wellness is gaining scientific legitimacy, moving from alternative health fringe to mainstream understanding. Consumers will increasingly expect products to fit within integrated wellness systems.

**Societal layer:** Control / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Position beverages as part of integrated wellness routines rather than isolated consumption moments. Partner with sleep, movement, and nutrition brands for ecosystem plays.

**What to monitor:** Cross-category wellness partnerships; consumer adoption of tracking and integration tools; scientific communication in mainstream media.

---

### SIGNAL 7: Class Complexity in Identity Politics

**What happened:** Symeon Brown's analysis of Black British professional class reveals tensions between visibility and representation—being prominent doesn't mean being representative of community experience.

**Why it is a signal:** Identity-based marketing faces increasing sophistication. Consumers recognise that surface diversity can mask class and experiential homogeneity. Authenticity requires deeper engagement.

**Societal layer:** Identity / belonging / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Diversity initiatives must go beyond visible representation to genuine community engagement. Working-class and regional voices matter as much as demographic categories.

**What to monitor:** Backlash against performative diversity; success of brands with genuine community roots; discourse around class in identity conversations.

---

### SIGNAL 8: The Body Commentary Exhaustion

**What happened:** Coverage of public discourse around Ariana Grande's body highlighted how "expressing concern" about celebrity health perpetuates harmful dynamics, with platforms unable to suppress engagement-driven content.

**Why it is a signal:** There's growing fatigue with body commentary disguised as health concern. Consumers increasingly reject wellness messaging that centres appearance, seeking function and feeling instead.

**Societal layer:** Identity / control / mental health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Avoid any marketing that implies body modification benefits. Focus on how products make people feel, not how they might make people look.

**What to monitor:** Backlash against "wellness" brands with weight-loss adjacency; success of body-neutral positioning; regulatory attention to health claims.

---

### SIGNAL 9: The Seasonal Ingredient Renaissance

**What happened:** Multiple food features emphasised peak seasonality, traditional techniques, and the science of flavour (tomato glutamate levels, proper storage) over novelty or convenience.

**Why it is a signal:** Sophisticated food culture is moving from "what's new" to "what's right"—proper technique, peak timing, and understanding why things taste good. This elevates craft and knowledge over trend-chasing.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Seasonal beverage programmes with genuine ingredient stories. Educate consumers on why timing and sourcing matter. Craft credentials require substance.

**What to monitor:** Consumer interest in ingredient provenance; success of seasonal-limited releases; growth of food education content.

---

### SIGNAL 10: Artist Identity Fluidity

**What happened:** The Weeknd reversed his retirement plans, acknowledging he "tested retirement" and didn't like it, while reflecting on having "mastered" his persona and seeking new challenges.

**Why it is a signal:** Even at the highest levels, identity is being treated as iterative rather than fixed. This normalises reinvention and gives permission for consumers to evolve their own self-presentation.

**Societal layer:** Identity / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brand identities can evolve without losing core audiences. Consumers understand and accept transformation when it's authentic rather than opportunistic.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to brand evolutions; success of "chapter" or "era" marketing frameworks; tolerance for inconsistency in brand voice.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Maintenance Menu

**Hypothesis:** Consumers will respond positively to beverage programmes explicitly framed around "maintenance" rather than "transformation"—daily rituals supporting ongoing wellbeing rather than promising change.

**Method:** Partner with three hospitality venues to introduce a "Maintenance Menu" section featuring functional non-alcoholic beverages with messaging focused on sustaining energy, calm, or focus throughout the day. Include subscription option for regulars. Track against standard menu items over 8 weeks.

**Success metric:** 25% higher repeat purchase rate for Maintenance Menu items versus standard functional beverages; qualitative feedback indicating resonance with "maintenance" framing.

---

### Experiment 2: The Visibility Venue Audit

**Hypothesis:** Venues optimised for "being seen" (good lighting, comfortable solo seating, reasons to linger) will outperform on dwell time and beverage sales among 18-25 consumers.

**Method:** Conduct environmental audits of 10 venues, scoring on "visibility factors" (lighting quality, sightlines, solo-friendly seating, phone-free zones). Correlate scores with dwell time and per-head spend data. Use findings to develop design guidelines for a pilot "SABSing-friendly" pop-up.

**Success metric:** Statistically significant correlation between visibility scores and under-25 dwell time; pilot venue achieves 40% higher average visit duration than comparable spaces.

---

### Experiment 3: The Nonordinary Occasions Programme

**Hypothesis:** Consumers are ready for beverages that acknowledge and support "nonordinary" states (deep calm, heightened presence, contemplative focus) without pathologising or over-promising.

**Method:** Develop three SKU concepts with experiential positioning language ("for moments of presence," "for deep listening," "for quiet clarity"). Test messaging and packaging with 500 consumers across age groups, measuring comfort with language, purchase intent, and perceived authenticity versus existing functional beverage positioning.

**Success metric:** Purchase intent parity with leading functional beverages; 30% higher "authenticity" scores; no significant discomfort with experiential language across demographics.
