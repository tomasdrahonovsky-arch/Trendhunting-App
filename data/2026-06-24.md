## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound recalibration of how people seek pleasure, presence, and meaning—particularly as external pressures intensify. The European heatwave forcing France to ban alcohol at public events isn't merely a safety measure; it's a visible marker of how environmental volatility is reshaping social rituals. When the Fête de la Musique—a celebration synonymous with wine and conviviality—must operate dry, we witness the fragility of alcohol-centric gathering culture.

Simultaneously, one-third of British adults have reduced alcohol consumption, driven not by health campaigns but by a simple "reduced desire to drink." This represents a fundamental identity shift: moderation is no longer a compromise but a preference. The rise of "zebra striping" (alternating between alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks) signals that consumers want optionality, not abstinence.

The hospitality industry is responding with experience-forward strategies. Estiatorio Milos' Greek-inspired spritz campaign sells "a Greek summer escape" rather than alcohol content. Solid Wiggles' artisanal Jell-O shots transform a fraternity cliché into a craft cocktail experience. Both demonstrate that the vessel and narrative matter as much as what's inside.

Meanwhile, mid-strength beer's resurgence in the US (4-5% ABV) and Japan's dominance of Australian premium imports reveal consumers seeking sophistication without excess. The cultural pendulum swings toward intentionality—people want to feel something specific, not just feel drunk.

For non-alcoholic beverage brands, this moment demands more than product innovation. It requires understanding that drinks are now identity markers, social technologies, and wellness tools. The winners will be those who design for presence, not just consumption.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: Forced Sobriety as Climate Adaptation

**What happened:** French authorities banned alcohol at state-organised events including Fête de la Musique as temperatures approached 42°C, citing combined risks of extreme heat, alcohol, and outdoor gatherings.

**Why it is a signal:** This marks the first major instance of climate conditions directly restricting alcohol consumption at scale in a wine-producing nation. It normalises the idea that environmental conditions can—and should—override drinking culture.

**Societal layer:** Control / community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic options become essential infrastructure for outdoor events, not optional alternatives. Brands positioned as "heat-appropriate" or "hydration-forward" gain legitimacy as default choices during extreme weather.

**What to monitor:** Municipal event policies in Mediterranean regions; insurance requirements for outdoor festivals; emergence of "climate-conscious" beverage categories.

---

### SIGNAL 2: The Desire Deficit

**What happened:** YouGov data shows 33% of British drinkers have reduced consumption, with the primary reason being "reduced desire to drink" (51%), outpacing financial concerns (33%) and health worries (29%).

**Why it is a signal:** This isn't about can't—it's about don't want to. The shift from external constraints to internal preference represents a generational identity change where sobriety or moderation is a positive choice, not a sacrifice.

**Societal layer:** Identity / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Marketing must shift from "you can still have fun without alcohol" to "this is what you actually want." Products should be positioned as first choices, not substitutes.

**What to monitor:** Language shifts in how moderation is discussed; dating app behaviour around drinking preferences; workplace social event formats.

---

### SIGNAL 3: The Zebra Stripe Becomes Mainstream

**What happened:** KAM Insight data reveals 48% of pub-goers now "zebra stripe" (alternate between alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks), with 56% believing alcohol-free beer is becoming mainstream in the UK.

**Why it is a signal:** This behaviour pattern legitimises non-alcoholic drinks as part of a drinking occasion, not separate from it. The binary of "drinker" vs "non-drinker" is dissolving into a spectrum of intentional consumption.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Venues need paired menus and staff trained to recommend NA options mid-session. Brands should design for "the second drink" occasion—when someone switches from alcohol to NA.

**What to monitor:** Pour ratios at bars tracking NA vs alcoholic orders per customer; emergence of "session planning" apps or tools.

---

### SIGNAL 4: The Elevated Jell-O Shot Renaissance

**What happened:** Brooklyn's Solid Wiggles has transformed Jell-O shots from fraternity cliché into artisanal cocktail experiences, now stocked in upscale NYC bars. LA's Vandell and other venues are following suit with aestheticised, sophisticated jelly cocktails.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents the "craft-ification" of previously lowbrow formats—taking something associated with excess and transforming it into an object of design and intention. It's about reclaiming pleasure through elevation.

**Societal layer:** Status / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Format innovation matters as much as liquid innovation. Non-alcoholic brands should explore unexpected delivery mechanisms—gels, frozen formats, edible vessels—that create Instagram moments and tactile experiences.

**What to monitor:** Patent filings for novel beverage formats; crossover between confectionery and beverage categories; "adult" versions of childhood treats.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Mid-Strength Beer's Quiet Comeback

**What happened:** US craft breweries are rebuilding around 4-5% ABV beers to distinguish from both light lagers and non-alcoholic options, inspired by drinking cultures in the UK, Scandinavia, and Australia where tax incentives favour lower-alcohol production.

**Why it is a signal:** This creates a "third way" between full-strength and zero-proof—acknowledging that many consumers want some alcohol effect without the commitment of higher ABV. It's moderation without abstinence.

**Societal layer:** Control / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** The competitive landscape isn't just NA vs alcoholic—it's a spectrum. NA brands must articulate their unique value proposition against mid-strength options that offer "just enough" alcohol.

**What to monitor:** Tax policy discussions around alcohol gradients; craft brewery ABV trends; consumer language around "sessionability."

---

### SIGNAL 6: Destination-in-a-Glass Marketing

**What happened:** Estiatorio Milos launched "Meet Me at Milos" Greek island-inspired spritzes, while AIX Rosé partnered with Playlister for curated summer soundtracks—both selling "accessible escape" as travel costs rise.

**Why it is a signal:** Beverages are being positioned as transportation devices—not to intoxication, but to place and feeling. This responds to economic constraints on travel while satisfying wanderlust through sensory experience.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands can own the "escape" narrative without the alcohol. Pairing drinks with playlists, scents, or visual experiences creates multi-sensory "micro-vacations" that don't require a passport or a hangover.

**What to monitor:** Collaborations between beverage brands and travel/lifestyle platforms; "destination" flavour profiles in product development; experiential retail concepts.

---

### SIGNAL 7: Japan's Premium Beer Dominance in Australia

**What happened:** Japan has overtaken Mexico as Australia's main source of imported beer, with premium international options consistently outperforming domestic value macros in urban hubs. Domestic brewers are responding by creating "local interpretations of global styles."

**Why it is a signal:** This reflects a broader pattern where "international identity" confers premium status. Consumers are paying for cultural cachet and perceived sophistication, not just liquid quality.

**Societal layer:** Status / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Origin stories and cultural authenticity drive premiumisation. NA brands should consider how geographic or cultural positioning can elevate perceived value—Japanese precision, Scandinavian minimalism, Mediterranean conviviality.

**What to monitor:** Import/export data for premium beverages; "localised global" product launches; demographic correlations with international brand preferences.

---

### SIGNAL 8: Star Bathing and Presence-Seeking Wellness

**What happened:** "Star bathing"—the practice of mindfully gazing at stars without technology—has emerged as a budget-friendly wellness trend, promoted for its ability to calm the nervous system and induce awe.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents the continued search for low-cost, accessible ways to achieve presence and mental reset. It's part of a broader movement away from consumption-based wellness toward attention-based practices.

**Societal layer:** Mental health / spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Beverages that support presence rather than escape will resonate. Consider products designed for "wind-down" rituals, stargazing occasions, or technology-free moments. Packaging and marketing should evoke stillness, not stimulation.

**What to monitor:** Growth of "slow" lifestyle content; evening ritual product categories; outdoor hospitality concepts designed for contemplation.

---

### SIGNAL 9: Heatwave Hospitality Adaptation

**What happened:** Jeremy Clarkson's pub The Farmer's Dog closed its butcher's counter due to extreme heat, warned customers about bringing dogs, and acknowledged that "certain things stop working properly" at 35°C+.

**Why it is a signal:** Climate volatility is forcing hospitality venues to publicly acknowledge operational limitations and prioritise customer/animal welfare over service completeness. Transparency about constraints is becoming a trust-building tool.

**Societal layer:** Community / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Heat-resilient products and services become competitive advantages. Brands should consider shelf stability, serving temperature flexibility, and "cooling" functional benefits as product development priorities.

**What to monitor:** Hospitality insurance claims related to weather; venue design innovations for temperature extremes; "heat-proof" product certifications.

---

### SIGNAL 10: The Premiumisation Imperative in Wine Distribution

**What happened:** Susana Balbo appointed Matthew Clark Bibendum as UK distribution partner with explicit focus on "premiumisation and growth across multiple channels," while Bordeaux en primeur analysis suggests distribution control matters more than price reduction.

**Why it is a signal:** Even in challenged categories, the path forward is premiumisation and channel strategy, not discounting. This reflects broader consumer willingness to pay more for fewer, better experiences.

**Societal layer:** Status / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands should resist the temptation to compete on price. Instead, invest in premium positioning, selective distribution, and channel-specific strategies that protect brand equity.

**What to monitor:** Distribution partnership announcements in NA category; price point trends for premium NA products; on-trade vs off-trade growth differentials.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Climate-Adaptive Beverage Menu

**Hypothesis:** Venues that offer weather-responsive menus—automatically featuring hydrating, lower-alcohol, or non-alcoholic options during heat alerts—will see increased customer satisfaction and per-head spend compared to static menus.

**Method:** Partner with 3-5 outdoor hospitality venues to implement dynamic digital menus that shift recommendations based on real-time temperature data. Track customer selections, satisfaction scores, and total spend across weather conditions over one summer season.

**Success metric:** 15% increase in NA/low-alcohol selection during heat events; 10% improvement in customer satisfaction scores; maintained or increased average transaction value.

---

### Experiment 2: The Second Drink Moment

**Hypothesis:** Positioning a non-alcoholic beverage specifically for "the second drink" occasion—when zebra-striping consumers switch from alcohol—will outperform general NA marketing in on-trade settings.

**Method:** Develop point-of-sale materials and staff training scripts that prompt NA recommendations after a customer's first alcoholic drink. A/B test against standard NA promotion in 10 matched venue pairs over 8 weeks.

**Success metric:** 25% increase in NA orders as second drinks in test venues; positive staff feedback on ease of recommendation; customer qualitative feedback indicating the prompt felt helpful rather than intrusive.

---

### Experiment 3: The Micro-Vacation Kit

**Hypothesis:** Bundling a premium non-alcoholic beverage with a curated playlist, scent element, and serving ritual guide will command a 40%+ price premium over the beverage alone and drive higher repeat purchase rates.

**Method:** Create three "destination" kits (Mediterranean, Nordic, Japanese) each containing an NA beverage, QR-linked playlist, small aromatic element (herb sachet, essential oil), and ritual card. Sell through DTC and measure against standalone beverage sales.

**Success metric:** 40% price premium achieved with maintained conversion rate; 20% higher repeat purchase rate for kit buyers vs standalone buyers; social sharing rate of 15%+ among purchasers.
