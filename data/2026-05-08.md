## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a fundamental recalibration of what "premium" means across beverages, experiences, and identity itself. The dominant thread is **intentional moderation as aspiration**—not deprivation, but sophisticated calibration. Olympic champion Jessica Ennis-Hill launching a 7% ABV Provence rosé crystallises this moment: elite performers don't abstain, they optimise. The product exists because "balance" has become the new performance metric for life, not just sport.

Simultaneously, we're witnessing the **resurrection of nightlife** as a deliberate counter-movement to digital exhaustion. London's Lost club—with its phone-locking policy and theatrical entry rituals—signals that physical presence is becoming the new luxury. This isn't nostalgia; it's a conscious rejection of ambient connectivity in favour of bounded, embodied experience.

The heritage beverage sector is responding to these shifts with unprecedented agility. Bordeaux's pivot toward Crémant (up 35% in exports), lighter claret styles, and organic/vegan expressions demonstrates that even the most tradition-bound categories recognise that modern consumers want provenance without heaviness. AB InBev's 27% growth in no-alcohol beer and 37% in "Beyond Beer" confirms this isn't a niche—it's the new centre.

Underneath these consumption shifts lies a deeper cultural current: **Gen Z's embrace of intuition and spirituality** as legitimate knowledge systems. When young people claim psychic abilities and seek meaning through tarot and embodied knowing, they're signalling distrust of institutional expertise and hunger for personal agency. For beverage brands, this means functional claims must feel discovered, not prescribed.

The strategic imperative is clear: the winning position is "sophisticated restraint"—products and experiences that signal discernment through what they exclude, not just what they contain.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Elite Athlete as Moderation Ambassador

**What happened:** Dame Jessica Ennis-Hill launched Seven Summers, a 7% ABV dealcoholised Provence rosé, explicitly framed around "balance" as an athletic and life philosophy. The product was developed with a Master of Wine to ensure quality parity with full-strength alternatives.

**Why it is a signal:** This marks a shift from celebrity alcohol endorsements (Casamigos, Aviation Gin) to celebrity moderation endorsements. When elite performers attach their personal brand to reduced-alcohol products, moderation gains aspirational status rather than being coded as restriction or health anxiety.

**Societal layer:** Status / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Mid-strength (3-8% ABV) represents an underdeveloped category with massive potential. Position these products as "performance optimisation" rather than "alcohol reduction." Partner with athletes, executives, and high-performers who embody intentional living.

**What to monitor:** Athlete and influencer partnerships in the low/no space; premium pricing acceptance for mid-strength wines; language shifts from "low alcohol" to "calibrated" or "precision."

### SIGNAL 2: Phone-Free Nightlife as Premium Experience

**What happened:** London's Lost club has become the city's most talked-about venue by locking phones in pouches at entry. The venue employs theatrical entry rituals and small-group experiences, creating what Dazed describes as a conscious rejection of documented nightlife.

**Why it is a signal:** After years of venues struggling against smartphone culture, a successful model has emerged that makes disconnection the product itself. This represents nightlife's evolution from "place to be seen" to "place to be present."

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Venues prioritising presence over documentation create ideal environments for premium non-alcoholic offerings—guests are there for the experience, not the content. Develop "undocumented" tasting experiences and phone-free service rituals that reward presence.

**What to monitor:** Phone-free policy adoption rates; premium pricing correlation with disconnection policies; emergence of "presence-first" hospitality concepts.

### SIGNAL 3: Bordeaux's Lightness Pivot

**What happened:** Bordeaux announced strategic diversification including Crémant growth (up 35% in export volume), repositioned "claret" as lighter and fruit-forward, and highlighted organic, vegan, and "atypical" blends including 100% Sémillon expressions.

**Why it is a signal:** When the world's most tradition-bound wine region actively promotes lighter styles and alternative formats, it confirms that "sessionability" and "approachability" have moved from trend to structural expectation. The old guard is adapting rather than resisting.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Heritage and provenance remain powerful, but must be decoupled from heaviness and intensity. Develop non-alcoholic offerings with genuine terroir stories and regional specificity. The "serious but light" positioning is wide open.

**What to monitor:** Premium wine regions launching lower-ABV or dealcoholised expressions; sommelier attitudes toward lighter styles; fine dining adoption of mid-strength pairings.

### SIGNAL 4: Gen Z's Intuition Economy

**What happened:** Dazed reports on Gen Z's embrace of psychic abilities and intuitive knowledge, with young people claiming predictive experiences and treating spiritual practices as legitimate epistemology rather than entertainment.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a fundamental shift in how a generation relates to knowledge and decision-making—away from institutional expertise toward embodied, personal knowing. It's not ironic or aesthetic; it's sincere.

**Societal layer:** Spirituality / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional benefits must feel intuited, not prescribed. Move away from clinical claims toward "listen to your body" positioning. Create products that support rituals of self-attunement rather than promising specific outcomes.

**What to monitor:** Tarot and astrology integration in wellness brands; "intuitive eating/drinking" language adoption; rejection of quantified-self approaches among younger consumers.

### SIGNAL 5: Immersive Theatre as Brand Activation Model

**What happened:** Hendrick's Gin launched "Anotherland" at Selfridges—a multi-sensory theatrical experience developed by the former chief storyteller of Sleep No More. The activation uses small-group guided narratives rather than traditional sampling.

**Why it is a signal:** Brand experiences are evolving from "try the product" to "enter the world." This represents the maturation of experiential marketing into genuine entertainment, where the brand becomes a portal rather than a pitch.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands have an advantage here—experiences can be longer, more complex, and more participatory when intoxication isn't a factor. Invest in narrative-driven activations that create shareable memories rather than shareable content.

**What to monitor:** Theatre company partnerships with beverage brands; ticket-price tolerance for brand experiences; emergence of "brand worlds" as entertainment category.

### SIGNAL 6: Ancient Ingredients, Modern Positioning

**What happened:** Mastiha, a Greek resin-based spirit with 2,500 years of documented history, is gaining momentum with bartenders globally. The spirit carries both historical medicinal claims and current European Medicines Agency approval for digestive benefits.

**Why it is a signal:** The "functional heritage" category is emerging—ingredients with ancient provenance that also satisfy modern wellness expectations. This bridges the gap between craft authenticity and health-conscious consumption.

**Societal layer:** Spirituality / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Source ingredients with documented historical use and legitimate functional properties. The combination of "ancient wisdom" and "modern validation" is particularly resonant. Develop non-alcoholic expressions of heritage spirits.

**What to monitor:** Bartender adoption of heritage spirits; regulatory pathways for functional claims; consumer response to "ancient remedy" positioning.

### SIGNAL 7: Digital Commerce as Primary Channel

**Societal layer:** Control / Community

**What to monitor:** D2C revenue as percentage of total sales; subscription model adoption in beverages; data partnership valuations.

### SIGNAL 8: The Homelessness Visibility Crisis

**What happened:** A vigil in Sydney's Hyde Park mourned Bikram Lama, a 32-year-old homeless man who died and lay undiscovered for up to a week in the city centre. The gathering emphasised that "no one should be left to die unseen."

**Why it is a signal:** Urban invisibility is becoming a moral flashpoint. As cities gentrify and public spaces become contested, the treatment of vulnerable populations reflects broader questions about who belongs and who is seen.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Hospitality brands operating in urban centres face increasing scrutiny about their relationship to public space and vulnerable communities. Authentic community investment—not performative charity—is becoming a reputational necessity.

**What to monitor:** Local community partnership expectations; "third place" positioning authenticity; staff training on vulnerable population interactions.

**What happened:** The Guardian's wine column addressed the challenge of pairing wine with vegetable-centric diets, noting that traditional "gutsy" reds are losing relevance as meat consumption declines.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Calibration Menu

**Hypothesis:** Consumers will pay premium prices for a curated "calibration menu" that offers the same base beverage at multiple ABV levels (0%, 3.5%, 7%, full strength), allowing them to choose their desired state rather than their desired drink.

**Method:** Partner with three upscale restaurants to offer a signature cocktail or wine in four ABV variants, priced identically. Track which variants are ordered, at what times, and in what social configurations. Conduct exit interviews about decision factors.

**Success metric:** 40%+ of orders choosing sub-full-strength options; willingness to pay equal or higher prices for reduced-ABV variants; repeat ordering behaviour indicating intentional calibration rather than one-time curiosity.

---

### Experiment 2: The Undocumented Tasting

**Hypothesis:** A phone-free, narrative-driven tasting experience for non-alcoholic beverages will generate higher Net Promoter Scores, longer dwell times, and greater purchase intent than traditional sampling activations.

**Method:** Create a 45-minute immersive tasting experience for a premium non-alcoholic portfolio. Phones locked at entry. Guided by trained "hosts" through a three-act narrative structure. Compare metrics against a control group experiencing traditional counter sampling of identical products.

**Success metric:** 25%+ higher NPS; 3x longer engagement time; 50%+ higher same-day purchase conversion; qualitative feedback emphasising "memory" over "content."

---

### Experiment 3: The Intuition Blend

**Hypothesis:** A non-alcoholic functional beverage positioned around "supporting your intuition" (using adaptogens with historical use for mental clarity) will outperform identical formulations positioned around "cognitive enhancement" among consumers under 30.

**Method:** Develop identical functional beverages with two distinct brand identities: one clinical/performance-focused ("Clarity+"), one intuition/spiritual-focused ("Inner Knowing"). A/B test across digital channels with identical media spend. Track click-through, conversion, repeat purchase, and social sharing.

**Success metric:** Intuition positioning achieves 30%+ higher engagement and 20%+ higher repeat purchase among under-30 demographic; qualitative research confirms emotional resonance with "listening to yourself" framing.
