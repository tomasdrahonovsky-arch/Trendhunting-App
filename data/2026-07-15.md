## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a fundamental recalibration of how consumers relate to pleasure, moderation, and identity—with significant implications for beverage brands navigating the post-alcohol-dominance era.

The most consequential signal emerges from IWSR data definitively debunking the "Gen Z moderation myth." With 74% of legal drinking age Gen Z now consuming alcohol—nearly identical to the broader population—the industry's foundational assumption about generational abstinence collapses. More striking: Baby Boomers are now the true moderation generation, with the lowest participation rates and fewest drinking occasions. This inverts conventional wisdom and demands strategic recalibration.

Meanwhile, South Korea offers a preview of mature moderation markets: non-alcoholic beer spending up 32.6% in three years, sober raves proliferating, and traditional soju volumes declining 13.4% since 2019. Notably, male consumers have overtaken women in NA beer spending—signaling moderation's movement from niche wellness behavior to mainstream masculine acceptability.

The wine industry's embrace of "back sweetening" represents a broader accessibility movement: established categories softening their edges to meet consumers where they are rather than demanding palate education. This "friendlier" positioning parallels the hospitality industry's evolution, evidenced by Mendoza's winery restaurants now comprising nearly half of Argentina's Michelin stars—collapsing the distinction between drinking occasion and dining experience.

Regulatory pressure intensifies as UK MPs push to ban fast food near schools and restrict junk food advertising, signaling an environment where "health-adjacent" positioning becomes both opportunity and minefield.

For non-alcoholic beverage builders, the moment demands abandoning generational stereotypes, embracing flavor accessibility without sacrificing sophistication, and recognizing that moderation is becoming demographically universal rather than youth-specific.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Gen Z Moderation Myth Collapses

**What happened:** IWSR's Bevtrac survey reveals 74% of legal drinking age Gen Z now consumes alcohol, up from 66% three years ago, nearly matching the 76% overall adult participation rate. Baby Boomers, at 71% and declining, are now the least-drinking generation.

**Why it is a signal:** This fundamentally inverts the industry's core narrative. The assumption that younger consumers would drive permanent category decline was shaping billions in investment decisions. The data suggests moderation is a life-stage phenomenon, not a generational identity.

**Societal layer:** Identity / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands positioned exclusively as "Gen Z alternatives" must broaden their demographic targeting. The real growth opportunity may be Boomers seeking health-conscious options without sacrificing social ritual. Hospitality venues should ensure NA offerings appeal across age groups, not just to presumed younger abstainers.

**What to monitor:** Boomer-focused wellness media; retirement community beverage programs; health-motivated moderation versus identity-motivated abstinence in consumer research.

---

### SIGNAL 2: South Korea's Sober Rave Economy

**What happened:** South Korean spending on non-alcoholic beer reached £39m (up 32.6% in three years), sober raves are emerging in major cities, and soju shipments have declined 13.4% since 2019. Male consumers now outspend women on NA beer.

**Why it is a signal:** South Korea's drinking culture has historically been among the world's most intense, with workplace drinking deeply embedded. This shift represents moderation penetrating even high-pressure drinking cultures—and crucially, becoming acceptable for men.

**Societal layer:** Rebellion / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** The gender flip in NA consumption suggests moderation is shedding its "wellness feminine" coding. Brands should develop positioning that speaks to masculine social contexts without health-washing. Sober nightlife concepts deserve serious investment attention.

**What to monitor:** Korean beauty and lifestyle exports (K-culture often predicts Western trends); workplace drinking policy changes in Asian markets; sober nightlife concepts in other high-drinking cultures.

---

### SIGNAL 3: Wine's "Friendlier" Sweetness Pivot

**What happened:** US wines by the glass are becoming sweeter through "back sweetening," with French regions including Côtes du Rhône seeking government approval for reds containing up to 9g/l sugar. The trend prioritizes accessibility over traditional dry wine orthodoxy.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents established categories abandoning gatekeeping in favor of meeting consumer preferences. The wine industry's willingness to modify centuries of tradition signals a broader shift toward flavor accessibility across premium beverages.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages can embrace sweetness strategically without apology—the "serious beverage" category is itself softening. However, transparency becomes critical: consumers increasingly expect to know what they're drinking. Consider sweetness-level labeling as a differentiation opportunity.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to sweetness transparency in wine; craft soda and botanical drink sweetness levels; backlash from purist communities.

---

### SIGNAL 4: Winery-Restaurant Convergence

**What happened:** Mendoza winery restaurants now account for six of Argentina's 14 Michelin-starred establishments. The Fladgate Partnership is rebranding as "Fladgate Family Wines" to emphasize its expansion beyond Port into still wines across Portuguese regions.

**Why it is a signal:** The boundary between beverage brand and hospitality experience continues dissolving. Producers are becoming destination operators; drinking occasions are becoming dining experiences.

**Societal layer:** Status / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverage brands should consider hospitality extensions—pop-up experiences, branded spaces, culinary partnerships. The product-as-destination model offers margin expansion and brand depth impossible through retail alone.

**What to monitor:** Beverage brand hospitality investments; experiential retail concepts; culinary partnership announcements from drinks companies.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Regulatory Pressure on "Unhealthy" Categories Intensifies

**What happened:** UK MPs recommended banning fast food outlets near schools, ending junk food advertising on billboards and public transport, and forcing supermarkets to display healthy products prominently. Separate commentary called for protecting public health policies from industry lobbying.

**Why it is a signal:** The regulatory environment for anything positioned as "unhealthy" is tightening. Categories adjacent to health concerns face increasing scrutiny, while "health-positive" positioning gains protective value.

**Societal layer:** Control / community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional and wellness-positioned beverages gain regulatory tailwind. However, health claims face increasing scrutiny—substantiation becomes essential. Brands should audit marketing claims for defensibility and consider proactive transparency as competitive advantage.

**What to monitor:** UK food policy implementation; EU health claim regulations; US FTC enforcement patterns on wellness marketing.

---

### SIGNAL 6: Cannabis Beverages Mature as Beer Alternatives

**What happened:** Cann Social Tonic is being positioned explicitly as a "beer replacement" for health-conscious consumers, with low-dose THC/CBD (2mg/4mg), 30-40 calories, and sophisticated flavor profiles (Yuzu Elderflower, Ginger Lemongrass).

**Why it is a signal:** Cannabis beverages are moving from novelty to functional category—specifically targeting the "weeknight beer" occasion with calorie and health positioning rather than intoxication intensity.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages compete not just with alcohol but with cannabis alternatives for the "relaxation without consequences" occasion. Functional ingredients (adaptogens, nootropics) that deliver mood benefits without cannabis's legal complexity become more valuable.

**What to monitor:** Cannabis beverage distribution expansion; state-by-state legalization; mainstream retailer adoption; consumer crossover between NA and cannabis beverages.

---

### SIGNAL 7: Brown-Forman Leadership Vacuum Signals Category Stress

**What happened:** Brown-Forman CEO Lawson Whiting announced retirement after a turbulent period including failed Pernod Ricard merger talks, rejected Sazerac bid, tariff impacts, and declining bourbon performance. The company was "accused of being slow off the mark in moving into RTDs."

**Why it is a signal:** Leadership instability at major spirits companies reflects category-wide stress. The explicit criticism about RTD tardiness confirms that format innovation—not just brand building—is now existential for traditional spirits companies.

**Societal layer:** Status / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Traditional spirits companies will accelerate NA and RTD acquisitions/partnerships. Independent NA brands should prepare for increased acquisition interest while building defensible brand equity. Format innovation (RTD, functional, occasion-specific) matters as much as liquid quality.

**What to monitor:** Spirits company M&A activity; RTD launch velocity; NA brand acquisition announcements.

---

### SIGNAL 8: Single-Origin Premiumization Reaches Champagne

**What happened:** Champagne Barons de Rothschild launched single-site expressions from grands crus in Avize and Cramant, with extremely low dosage (2-3g/l), old vine sourcing, and specific terroir storytelling including historical references (plague hospital sites).

**Why it is a signal:** Even Champagne—the ultimate blended category—is embracing single-origin storytelling. Provenance specificity and production transparency are becoming universal premiumization tools.

**Societal layer:** Status / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages can borrow premiumization language from wine: single-origin botanicals, specific harvest storytelling, terroir-equivalent concepts for tea, coffee, or botanical sourcing. Low-intervention and minimal-dosage positioning translates directly.

**What to monitor:** Botanical sourcing transparency in NA brands; single-origin claims in functional beverages; wine-adjacent positioning in premium NA.

### SIGNAL 9: Friendship Quality as Health Indicator

**What happened:** Psychology Today and academic research highlighted that imbalanced friendships correlate with worse mental health outcomes, with specific behavioral markers (always initiating, conversational narcissism, post-interaction depletion) identified as warning signs.

**Why it is a signal:** Relationship quality is being medicalized and measured. Social connection is moving from soft cultural value to hard health metric, creating demand for experiences and products that facilitate genuine connection.

**Societal layer:** Mental health / community / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Hospitality concepts that facilitate genuine connection (not just co-presence) gain health-adjacent positioning. NA beverages can position around "quality time" rather than "good time"—enabling presence rather than escape.

**What to monitor:** Loneliness epidemic coverage; friendship app development; hospitality concepts emphasizing connection depth over volume.

---

### SIGNAL 10: Eater's World's Fare Signals Food-as-Unifier Positioning

**What happened:** Eater announced its largest-ever culinary event, World's Fare, explicitly positioning food as a "universal unifier" during international soccer events, with family-friendly programming and global cuisine representation.

**Why it is a signal:** Major media brands are investing in physical experiences that position food/beverage as cultural bridge-building rather than mere consumption. The explicit framing around unity and diversity reflects demand for gathering occasions with meaning.

**Societal layer:** Community / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Event and festival partnerships offer NA brands access to "unifying" positioning. Family-friendly contexts (where alcohol is inappropriate) represent natural NA occasions. Cultural celebration events provide storytelling opportunities for globally-sourced ingredients.

**What to monitor:** Food festival programming; family-friendly hospitality concepts; cultural celebration event beverage partnerships.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: Boomer Moderation Club

**Hypothesis:** Baby Boomers represent an underserved market for premium NA beverages, seeking health-conscious options without sacrificing sophistication or social ritual. A subscription service with educational content and community features will achieve higher retention than youth-targeted NA subscriptions.

**Method:** Launch a 90-day pilot subscription targeting adults 55+ through health-focused media partnerships (AARP, wellness newsletters). Include monthly curated NA selections, food pairing guides, and virtual tasting events. Position around "wisdom drinking"—choosing quality over quantity. Track against a control group of 25-40 subscribers receiving identical products with youth-oriented branding.

**Success metric:** 60-day retention rate 15% higher than control group; Net Promoter Score above 50; average order value increase of 20% by month three.

---

### Experiment 2: Sober Social Club Pop-Up Series

**Hypothesis:** Dedicated sober nightlife experiences will attract consumers across genders and age groups when positioned around quality experience rather than abstinence identity, generating premium pricing acceptance and brand loyalty.

**Method:** Partner with three venues in different markets (urban nightlife district, suburban entertainment center, resort/destination) to host monthly "Social Club" evenings featuring NA cocktail menus, live music, and facilitated social activities. No alcohol-free messaging in marketing—position purely on experience quality. Collect demographic data and post-event surveys on willingness to pay, likelihood to return, and brand perception.

**Success metric:** Gender balance within 40/60 split; age distribution spanning at least three decades; 70% of attendees willing to pay premium pricing ($15+ for NA cocktails); 50% expressing intent to return.

---

### Experiment 3: Terroir Transparency Label System

**Hypothesis:** Wine-style provenance storytelling (single-origin botanicals, harvest dates, production methods) will command 25%+ price premium for NA beverages among consumers who currently purchase premium wine or craft spirits.

**Method:** Develop three SKUs of an existing NA botanical beverage with enhanced labeling: specific botanical sourcing location, harvest date, production batch size, and maker notes. A/B test against standard labeling in specialty retail and DTC channels. Survey purchasers on purchase motivation and perceived value.

**Success metric:** 25% price premium acceptance; 40% of purchasers citing provenance information as purchase factor; repeat purchase rate 20% higher than standard-labeled product.
