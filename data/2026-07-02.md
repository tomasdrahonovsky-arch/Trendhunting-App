## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between heritage and reinvention, as institutions and individuals alike grapple with how to honour tradition while adapting to radically shifted consumer expectations. The 250th anniversary of America prompts reflection on wine's diminished role in national identity—a symbolic marker of broader beverage category fragmentation. Meanwhile, organic wine's decline in France (-2.4%) signals that ethical positioning alone no longer guarantees market success; consumers demand both values alignment AND experiential delivery.

The World Cup's arrival in North America is catalysing unexpected cultural crossovers—from dating opportunities to culinary events to fashion collaborations—demonstrating how major sporting moments create permission structures for new social behaviours and brand activations. Eater's "World's Fare" event exemplifies how food and beverage experiences are becoming the connective tissue for community formation around shared cultural moments.

A quieter but significant shift emerges in how people relate to consumption itself. The rise of "intentional listening" in music—deliberately choosing albums over algorithmic shuffle—mirrors broader desires for curated, meaningful experiences over passive consumption. This intentionality extends to hospitality, where fine wine retailers like Westgarth emphasise "authentic connection to producers and stories behind labels" rather than mere transaction.

The health landscape continues its complexity: statins have effectively normalised cholesterol levels across weight categories, potentially disrupting wellness-based beverage positioning that relies on cardiovascular health claims. Meanwhile, tick-borne disease concerns may reshape outdoor hospitality and picnic culture.

For non-alcoholic beverage brands, the strategic imperative is clear: category growth requires moving beyond substitution logic toward creating distinct cultural moments and identity markers that justify premium positioning and ritual adoption.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: Wine's Diminishing Role in National Identity

**What happened:** Karen MacNeil reflects on the 1986 "Toast to Liberty" when Americans collectively raised glasses of domestic wine for the Statue of Liberty's relighting, noting such unified wine moments seem impossible today—"it's hard to imagine a room full of American politicians all toasting with wine."

**Why it is a signal:** Wine has lost its position as a default celebratory beverage and unifying cultural ritual. The fragmentation of beverage choices reflects broader polarisation and the decline of shared ceremonial moments.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Opportunity exists to position premium NA beverages as new unifying ritual drinks—particularly for moments requiring inclusivity across drinking preferences. Brands can claim the "toast" occasion that wine is vacating.

**What to monitor:** Beverage choices at major civic events; political and corporate hospitality protocols; wedding and celebration trends.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Organic Credentials No Longer Sufficient

**What happened:** French organic wine sales fell 2.4% in 2025 despite overall organic food sector growth of 3.6%. Major wine regions Nouvelle-Aquitaine (-13% producers) and Occitanie (-5% producers) saw significant declines in organic production.

**Why it is a signal:** Ethical positioning has become table stakes rather than differentiator. Consumers expect sustainability AND superior experience, taste, or value. The "organic premium" is eroding.

**Societal layer:** Identity / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands cannot rely solely on health or sustainability messaging. Must layer functional benefits, taste excellence, and cultural relevance. "Better for you" needs "better for your experience."

**What to monitor:** Premium organic NA brand performance; consumer research on purchase drivers; sustainability messaging fatigue indicators.

---

### SIGNAL 3: Sporting Events as Social Permission Structures

**What happened:** The World Cup is generating unexpected cultural crossovers: Dazed reports Americans viewing it as a "once-in-a-lifetime dating opportunity," while Eater hosts a massive culinary event ("World's Fare") explicitly linking food culture to football fandom.

**Why it is a signal:** Major sporting events now function as permission structures for social behaviours beyond the sport itself—dating, culinary exploration, fashion experimentation. They create temporary communities with lowered social barriers.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** World Cup and similar events offer ideal activation moments for NA brands to establish new drinking occasions. The social permission extends to trying new beverages without judgment.

**What to monitor:** Social behaviour patterns during major sporting events; dating app data during tournaments; hospitality venue performance during matches.

---

### SIGNAL 4: The Rise of Intentional Consumption

**What happened:** A Dazed writer describes committing to listen to one new album daily throughout 2026, rejecting algorithmic shuffle for deliberate discovery. The piece frames this as recovering "the excitement for discovering new music" lost to passive consumption.

**Why it is a signal:** Backlash against algorithmic curation is manifesting as deliberate, effortful consumption rituals. People seek agency and meaning in their choices, not just convenience.

**Societal layer:** Control / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Opportunity for brands that reward intentional exploration—tasting flights, subscription discoveries, educational components. Position against "default" drinking behaviour.

**What to monitor:** Growth of subscription/discovery models; consumer language around "intentional" choices; anti-algorithm sentiment in adjacent categories.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Fine Wine's Pivot to Experiential Intimacy

**What happened:** Westgarth Wines' new European director emphasises that "today's collectors want an authentic connection to producers and the stories behind the labels," offering château dinners and bespoke tastings rather than mere transactions.

**Why it is a signal:** Even in traditional fine wine, the value proposition is shifting from product to experience and relationship. Provenance storytelling becomes the premium, not just liquid quality.

**Societal layer:** Status / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium NA brands must develop equivalent experiential layers—founder access, production visits, exclusive events. The bottle is the entry point, not the destination.

**What to monitor:** Fine wine retailer service evolution; luxury hospitality experience trends; collector community behaviours.

---

### SIGNAL 6: Women Redefining Technical Industries

**What happened:** Jane Renwick, first female board member at LCB, discusses 30 years challenging perceptions in drinks logistics, emphasising that "being a woman in logistics is a strength" and that skills like "attention to detail, organisation and strategic thinking" are increasingly valued.

**Why it is a signal:** Technical and operational roles in beverages are being reframed from masculine-coded to skill-based, opening pathways for diverse talent and potentially different product development approaches.

**Societal layer:** Identity / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brands should examine whether their operations, marketing, and product development reflect diverse perspectives. Female-led or female-focused NA brands may find receptive audiences.

**What to monitor:** Leadership diversity in beverage companies; female founder/operator success stories; consumer response to female-led brands.

### SIGNAL 7: Health Metrics Normalisation Disrupts Wellness Positioning

**What happened:** A study found that over-40s with obesity have "indistinguishable" cholesterol and blood pressure levels compared to healthy-weight individuals, largely due to statin use. Researchers noted people with obesity were sometimes "better off."

**Why it is a signal:** Pharmaceutical interventions are decoupling traditional health markers from lifestyle choices, potentially undermining beverage positioning based on cardiovascular or metabolic health claims.

**Societal layer:** Control / identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Health claims around heart health or cholesterol may lose resonance. Shift toward mental health, energy, sleep, gut health, or cognitive function where pharmaceutical solutions are less established.

**What to monitor:** Consumer health concern priorities; pharmaceutical pipeline for lifestyle conditions; functional beverage claim effectiveness research.

---

### SIGNAL 8: Outdoor Leisure Anxiety Emerging

**What happened:** The Guardian questions whether celebrities "rolling around in long grass" is safe given rising tick-borne Lyme disease, noting the images of Charli xcx and celebrity couples lounging in meadows alongside health warnings.

**Why it is a signal:** Nature-based leisure—picnics, outdoor dining, festival culture—may face new friction as disease awareness increases. The pastoral ideal meets health anxiety.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Outdoor hospitality concepts may need to address safety concerns explicitly. Indoor "nature-adjacent" experiences or controlled outdoor environments may gain appeal.

**What to monitor:** Outdoor event attendance trends; picnic culture evolution; nature-based hospitality concepts; health anxiety research.

---

### SIGNAL 9: Luxury Rosé Creates New Fine Wine Category

**What happened:** Patrick Schmitt MW profiles the emergence of fine rosé as a legitimate category, tracing it to Château d'Esclans' 2006 Garrus—a barrel-aged pink wine priced above £100 that proved rosé could be cellared and aged like great whites or reds.

**Why it is a signal:** Category boundaries in beverages continue to blur and elevate. What was once dismissed as casual summer drinking now commands fine wine treatment and pricing.

**Societal layer:** Status / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Precedent exists for elevating "casual" categories to premium status through production method innovation and storytelling. NA wines and aperitifs could follow similar trajectory.

**What to monitor:** Premium NA wine development; barrel-aged or complex production NA products; price ceiling testing in NA categories.

### SIGNAL 10: Nightlife as Brand Platform

**What happened:** Nothing (the tech company with Charli xcx as brand ambassador) launches "Club Nothing"—a nightlife series with a global fund supporting club culture, positioning the brand at "the epicentre of where music comes alive."

**Why it is a signal:** Non-endemic brands increasingly view nightlife and club culture as authentic brand-building platforms, not just sponsorship opportunities. The club becomes a brand expression space.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands have natural alignment with nightlife longevity (staying out longer, feeling better). Club partnerships and nightlife-native brand building offer differentiated positioning.

**What to monitor:** Brand-nightlife partnerships; sober-curious club nights; NA beverage presence in nightlife venues.

---

### SIGNAL 11: Artist Reliability as Brand Risk

**What happened:** Lil Wayne cancelled a Maine concert at the last minute without explanation, prompting furious fan responses including "This is EXACTLY why he didn't get hired for the Super Bowl" and speculation about reputation damage.

**Why it is a signal:** In an era of parasocial relationships and social media accountability, artist/influencer reliability becomes a measurable brand risk factor. Cancellations have amplified reputational consequences.

**Societal layer:** Community / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Influencer and artist partnerships require reliability assessment alongside reach metrics. Smaller, more reliable partners may outperform unreliable celebrities.

**What to monitor:** Influencer reliability metrics; cancellation/no-show tracking; fan community sentiment analysis.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Intentional Tasting Subscription

**Hypothesis:** Consumers experiencing "algorithmic fatigue" will pay premium prices for curated NA beverage discovery experiences that reward deliberate exploration over passive consumption.

**Method:** Launch a 12-week "Intentional Drinking" subscription featuring one premium NA beverage weekly, accompanied by producer stories, tasting notes, and suggested pairing rituals. Include a physical journal for recording impressions. Market explicitly as an antidote to default drinking habits.

**Success metric:** 40% subscriber retention beyond initial 12 weeks; Net Promoter Score above 50; social sharing of journal entries/tasting notes indicating ritual adoption.

---

### Experiment 2: World Cup Watch Party NA Activation

**Hypothesis:** Major sporting events create social permission for trying new beverages; NA brands can capture "toast moments" that wine is vacating by positioning as the inclusive celebration drink.

**Method:** Partner with 20 hospitality venues hosting World Cup viewing events to offer complimentary NA "match day toast" at kickoff of key games. Provide branded glassware and ritual script ("To the beautiful game"). Capture social content and measure subsequent NA menu orders.

**Success metric:** 25% of toast participants ordering NA beverages during match; 500+ social media posts using campaign hashtag; 15% increase in NA menu sales at participating venues during tournament.

---

### Experiment 3: Club Nothing-Style Nightlife Partnership

**Hypothesis:** NA beverages can build authentic brand equity through nightlife partnerships that support club culture rather than simply sponsoring it, capturing the "stay out longer, feel better" occasion.

**Method:** Create a small fund (£25,000) supporting three independent club nights in key cities, with NA beverage integration as the only "sponsorship" element—free product for attendees, branded but subtle presence. Document the nights through photography/video. Measure brand sentiment among attendees versus control group.

**Success metric:** 30% higher brand awareness among club night attendees; positive sentiment ratio above 4:1 in social mentions; three or more organic requests from other club promoters to participate.
