## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between control and chaos, heritage and disruption. The most significant signal emerges from South Korea, where whisky has overtaken wine at major retailers while non-alcoholic beverages have doubled their market share to 14%—a simultaneous embrace of at-home indulgence and mindful abstention. This bifurcation reflects a consumer base fragmenting along lines of intentionality rather than traditional demographics.

The hospitality sector faces mounting pressure from multiple directions: tipping disputes expose worker precarity, extreme climate events threaten supply chains, and the economics of drinking are shifting decisively toward home consumption. Meanwhile, legacy institutions—from Bordeaux châteaux celebrating century-old family ownership to British craft breweries consolidating under new management—are scrambling to articulate relevance in an era that increasingly values authenticity over pedigree.

A quieter but consequential thread runs through today's signals: the democratisation of expertise. Whether it's AI offering skincare diagnoses, coffee connoisseurs sharing precise brewing ratios, or Eater bringing food journalism to Netflix, knowledge that once required gatekeepers is becoming universally accessible. For beverage brands, this means consumers arrive more informed, more demanding, and less impressed by mystique alone.

The cultural moment is one of recalibration. Consumers are drinking less but caring more about what they drink. They're staying home but seeking connection. They're sceptical of institutions but hungry for heritage that feels earned rather than inherited. The brands that will thrive are those that understand this isn't contradiction—it's coherence. People want agency over their consumption, transparency in their transactions, and meaning in their rituals. The non-alcoholic beverage space sits precisely at this intersection.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Korean Bifurcation

**What happened:** At Lotte Mart, South Korea's major retailer, whisky overtook wine as the second-best-selling alcohol category while non-alcoholic beverages doubled their share from 6% to 14% since 2021. Non-alcoholic wine specifically grew 41.2%.

**Why it is a signal:** This isn't moderation—it's polarisation. Consumers are simultaneously trading up to premium spirits for intentional indulgence and opting out entirely. The middle ground of casual wine consumption is collapsing.

**Societal layer:** Control / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** The "better or nothing" mindset creates opportunity for premium NA offerings positioned as deliberate choices rather than compromises. Korean market data suggests NA wine has particular momentum—a category often dismissed in Western markets.

**What to monitor:** Korean convenience store NA assortments; home-drinking ritual content on Korean social platforms; premium NA pricing elasticity in Asian markets.

### SIGNAL 2: The Neighbourhood Intimacy Economy

**What happened:** Cantina Boccone opened in Deptford—a 16-seat wine bar and deli founded by partners from Rome and Lisbon, explicitly designed around "staying longer than you planned" and championing underrepresented Portuguese wines.

**Why it is a signal:** Post-pandemic hospitality is splitting between scalable concepts and hyper-local intimacy. This venue represents the latter: small, personal, rooted in founders' actual tastes rather than market research.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Intimate venues need NA options that reward lingering—drinks designed for extended stays, multiple rounds, and conversation. The "stay longer" ethos requires beverages that don't accelerate intoxication.

**What to monitor:** Independent venue openings emphasising seat count under 25; "neighbourhood" language in hospitality marketing; Portuguese wine bar expansion as a proxy for underdog regional interest.

### SIGNAL 3: Climate Pressure on Terroir Identity

**What happened:** French vineyards experienced 44°C temperatures, with one Loire Valley grower losing 40% of their crop to sunburnt grapes. Meanwhile, UK vineyard acreage grew 400% between 2004-2021 as conditions improve.

**Why it is a signal:** Terroir—the foundational concept of place-based authenticity—is becoming unstable. Traditional wine regions face existential threats while new regions emerge, disrupting centuries of geographic prestige.

**Societal layer:** Identity / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** As climate destabilises traditional provenance narratives, NA beverages can build new terroir stories around botanicals, water sources, and regional ingredients without the baggage of threatened vineyards.

**What to monitor:** Insurance costs for European vineyards; emerging wine regions' marketing language; consumer sentiment toward "new world" versus "old world" provenance claims.

---

### SIGNAL 4: The Consolidation of Craft

**What happened:** Great British Drinks Co appointed a new managing director to oversee eight acquired craft breweries, explicitly framing the move as "restoring regional British brewing" while accelerating growth.

**Why it is a signal:** Independent craft is being absorbed into portfolio companies that deploy "regional" and "craft" as marketing language while centralising operations. The authenticity premium is being industrialised.

**Societal layer:** Rebellion / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** As craft beer's independence narrative erodes, NA craft beverages can claim the authenticity mantle—genuinely small-batch, founder-led, and resistant to consolidation.

**What to monitor:** Craft brewery acquisition pace; consumer awareness of ownership structures; "truly independent" certification schemes.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Worker Precarity Surfaces

**What happened:** Unite called for withdrawal of the UK government's tipping code, arguing it fails to give hospitality workers control over gratuity distribution and could allow management to influence tip allocation.

**Why it is a signal:** Labour disputes in hospitality are becoming visible consumer issues. The ethics of where money goes—not just what you buy—is entering mainstream consciousness.

**Societal layer:** Control / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Transparency about worker treatment is becoming a brand differentiator. Beverage brands and venues that demonstrate equitable practices will attract values-aligned consumers.

**What to monitor:** Hospitality union activity; consumer boycott patterns; "fair wage" certification adoption in beverage retail.

### SIGNAL 6: The Democratisation of Expertise

**What happened:** The Guardian published detailed guidance on cafetière coffee technique, emphasising precise ratios, water quality, and grind size—knowledge previously gatekept by specialty coffee culture.

**Why it is a signal:** Expertise is being unbundled and distributed. Consumers increasingly expect to understand the "why" behind quality, not just trust brand authority.

**Societal layer:** Control / status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands must be prepared to educate transparently. Consumers will demand to know extraction methods, ingredient sourcing, and flavour science—not just marketing narratives.

**What to monitor:** Recipe transparency demands; "how it's made" content engagement; consumer willingness to invest in preparation equipment.

### SIGNAL 7: Food Media Goes Mainstream Entertainment

**What happened:** Eater announced a Netflix partnership, bringing food journalism series to one of the world's largest streaming platforms starting August 3.

**Why it is a signal:** Food and beverage content is graduating from niche interest to mainstream entertainment. The audience for culinary storytelling is expanding dramatically.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Beverage brands have an opportunity to participate in narrative-driven content. The appetite for drink-focused storytelling—origins, makers, rituals—is growing alongside food content.

**What to monitor:** Beverage-focused streaming content; food media viewership demographics; brand integration opportunities in culinary programming.

### SIGNAL 8: Subcultural Beauty as Identity Marker

**What happened:** Dazed documented beauty looks from My Chemical Romance's Glasgow show, capturing emo-goth aesthetics as fans used appearance to signal tribal belonging.

**Why it is a signal:** Subcultures continue to use aesthetic markers—including what they consume—as identity signals. Music scenes remain powerful organising forces for consumption patterns.

**Societal layer:** Identity / belonging / rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverages can align with subcultural aesthetics. Dark, dramatic, or countercultural visual identities may resonate with audiences who reject mainstream wellness positioning.

**What to monitor:** Music festival beverage preferences; subcultural influencer partnerships; aesthetic alignment between beverage branding and scene identities.

---

### SIGNAL 9: The Biohacking Paradox

**What happened:** Bryan Johnson, the tech entrepreneur spending millions on longevity protocols, was diagnosed with autoimmune gastritis—an incurable disease he attributes partly to childhood diet and chronic stress.

**Why it is a signal:** Even extreme optimisation cannot outrun biology. The limits of control are becoming visible, potentially tempering the most aggressive wellness narratives.

**Societal layer:** Control / mental health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** The "hack your way to health" narrative may be peaking. Beverages positioned around acceptance, balance, and pleasure—rather than optimisation—may find growing resonance.

**What to monitor:** Wellness influencer tone shifts; "anti-optimisation" content; consumer fatigue with functional claims.

---

### SIGNAL 10: Heritage as Living Practice

**What happened:** Château Brane-Cantenac unveiled a commemorative label celebrating 100 years of family ownership, explicitly framing heritage as "living" rather than static—a bridge between past and present.

**Why it is a signal:** Legacy brands are learning to articulate heritage as ongoing practice rather than museum piece. The language of "living heritage" suggests evolution within tradition.

**Societal layer:** Identity / spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands can build heritage narratives around botanical traditions, regional practices, or founder stories—but must frame them as evolving rather than frozen.

**What to monitor:** Heritage language in new brand launches; consumer response to "tradition" versus "innovation" framing; intergenerational brand storytelling.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Bifurcated Menu

**Hypothesis:** Venues offering explicitly "premium indulgence" and "mindful abstention" NA options—rather than a single NA category—will see higher NA attachment rates and average transaction values.

**Method:** Partner with three hospitality venues to restructure NA menus into two distinct sections: "Indulgence" (complex, rich, dessert-adjacent) and "Intention" (functional, clean, low-sugar). Track ordering patterns, dwell time, and customer feedback over 8 weeks.

**Success metric:** 20% increase in NA orders per cover; qualitative feedback indicating customers felt "understood" by the menu structure.

---

### Experiment 2: The Transparency Audit

**Hypothesis:** NA beverage brands that publish detailed production transparency (ingredient sourcing, extraction methods, worker conditions) will outperform competitors on purchase intent among values-driven consumers.

**Method:** Create two versions of product landing pages for an NA brand—one standard, one with comprehensive transparency documentation. A/B test with 2,000 consumers, measuring time on page, click-through to purchase, and post-purchase satisfaction.

**Success metric:** 15% higher conversion rate on transparency-enhanced pages; measurable increase in social sharing of transparency content.

---

### Experiment 3: The Anti-Optimisation Position

**Hypothesis:** An NA beverage positioned explicitly against wellness optimisation—emphasising pleasure, acceptance, and "enough"—will resonate with consumers experiencing functional fatigue.

**Method:** Develop campaign creative for an existing NA product that rejects functional claims entirely, focusing instead on sensory pleasure and present-moment enjoyment. Test against standard functional messaging with matched audiences across social platforms.

**Success metric:** Higher engagement rates (shares, saves, comments) on anti-optimisation creative; qualitative sentiment analysis showing relief or recognition in consumer responses.
