## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between authenticity and artifice—a battle playing out across beverages, real estate, and personal identity. The dominant signal is a consumer base increasingly sophisticated at detecting manufactured experiences, yet simultaneously craving the ritual and social scaffolding that traditional consumption occasions provide.

In beverages, we see this manifest in two parallel movements: the push for regulatory protection of "real" cider and perry (minimum 50% juice content) against industrial dilution, and the continued mainstreaming of non-alcoholic RTDs that promise the aperitivo ritual without the alcohol. These aren't contradictory—they're both expressions of a consumer demanding honesty about what they're consuming while refusing to sacrifice the social and sensory experience.

The moderation movement has definitively crossed from niche to norm. Molson Coors' language around "lasting shift" and "elevated experience" signals that major players now view mindful drinking as structural, not cyclical. The "zebra striping" behaviour—alternating between alcoholic and non-alcoholic serves—has become sufficiently mainstream to warrant dedicated product development.

Meanwhile, extreme heat events are reshaping consumption patterns in real-time. Western Europe's hottest June on record, with death tolls potentially exceeding 20,000, creates urgent demand for cooling, hydrating, and restorative beverages. The World Cup is simultaneously driving unprecedented on-premise traffic, with 8 million pints sold during a single England match—demonstrating that communal viewing occasions remain powerful consumption drivers.

The strategic imperative is clear: brands must deliver genuine substance (provenance, ingredients, craft) wrapped in sophisticated social ritual. The consumer will no longer accept one without the other.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Authenticity Regulation Movement

**What happened:** CAMRA launched an e-lobby campaign urging the UK government to raise minimum juice content in cider and perry from 35% to at least 50%, with 87% of consumers supporting the change in a YouGov poll.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents consumers weaponising regulation to protect against industrial dilution. The framing of "65% alcoholic sugar water" as deceptive marks a shift from passive preference to active advocacy for ingredient transparency.

**Societal layer:** Control / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands should pre-emptively adopt radical ingredient transparency as a competitive moat. Expect "real ingredients" certification schemes to emerge. Hospitality venues can differentiate by auditing and communicating the authenticity of their NA offerings.

**What to monitor:** EU regulatory discussions on similar standards; emergence of third-party "real ingredients" certification bodies; consumer advocacy groups expanding beyond alcohol to functional beverages.

### SIGNAL 3: Zebra Striping Goes Mainstream

**What happened:** Industry reports now casually reference "zebra striping"—alternating between alcoholic and non-alcoholic serves—as an established consumer behaviour driving product development.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Aperitivo Hour Programme

**Hypothesis:** Hospitality venues that create dedicated NA aperitivo programmes with proper glassware, garnishes, and premium pricing will see NA beverage revenue increase by 40% and overall beverage margin improve by 15%.

**Method:** Partner with 10 upscale bars/restaurants to implement a structured "Aperitivo Hour" featuring 3-4 premium NA options served in appropriate glassware with proper garnishes, priced at 70-80% of alcoholic equivalents. Train staff on NA recommendations as sophisticated choices. Track NA sales, overall beverage margin, and customer feedback over 90 days versus control venues.

**Success metric:** 40% increase in NA beverage revenue; 15% improvement in overall beverage margin; Net Promoter Score for NA offerings above 50.

---

### Experiment 2: The Heat Response Product Line

**Hypothesis:** A limited-edition "cooling botanicals" NA beverage line launched during heat events will achieve 3x the trial rate of standard product launches and establish brand association with climate-responsive innovation.

**Method:** Develop 2-3 SKUs featuring cooling botanicals (cucumber, mint, aloe) with electrolyte enhancement. Create rapid-deployment marketing assets triggered by heat warnings. Partner with 5 retailers in heat-affected regions for prominent placement during heat events. Measure trial rates, repeat purchase, and brand association surveys.

**Success metric:** 3x trial rate versus standard launches; 25% repeat purchase within 30 days; 40% of surveyed consumers associate brand with "climate-responsive" or "innovative."

### Experiment 3: The Radical Transparency Audit

**Hypothesis:** NA brands that publish complete ingredient sourcing documentation and production process videos will see 25% higher purchase intent among health-conscious consumers and 50% higher social sharing rates.

**Method:** Create comprehensive "ingredient journey" content for 3 NA products, including supplier interviews, production facility tours, and third-party testing results. A/B test marketing campaigns featuring transparency content versus standard product messaging. Survey purchase intent and track social sharing metrics.

**Success metric:** 25% higher purchase intent in transparency-exposed group; 50% higher social sharing rate; 30% increase in "trust" brand attribute scores.
