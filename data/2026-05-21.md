## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a drinks industry in profound transition, where authenticity, accessibility, and intentionality are displacing volume-driven narratives and passive consumption. The most significant signal emerges from France, where beer has overtaken wine consumption for the first time in modern history—a psychological milestone that reflects broader generational shifts toward casual, approachable drinking occasions rather than ritualistic formality.

Simultaneously, we observe a recalibration of what "premium" means. Wild Idol's expansion into fine dining venues demonstrates that alcohol-free options are no longer relegated to afterthought status but are earning their place through craftsmanship and chef collaboration. In Shanghai, the BUNCH natural wine festival succeeds precisely because it treats wine as social currency for "cool kids" rather than connoisseurship gatekeeping—people dress up for BUNCH in ways they never would for trade shows.

The worker-owned Sea & Soil Co-op in Brooklyn introduces sliding-scale pricing, explicitly acknowledging that accessibility and fair compensation can coexist. This represents a maturation of the "third space" concept beyond mere aesthetics toward genuine structural equity.

Meanwhile, regulatory pressure intensifies. The ASA's ruling against La Vieille Ferme's TikTok campaign—which linked wine to "emotional support" and "self-care"—signals that wellness-washing in alcohol marketing faces increasing scrutiny. This creates both constraint and opportunity for non-alcoholic brands positioned authentically within wellbeing spaces.

The throughline: consumers increasingly seek drinks that align with their values, identities, and social contexts rather than simply their taste preferences. Brands that understand drinking as a form of self-expression and community participation—rather than mere consumption—will capture the emerging cultural moment.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: France's Beer-Over-Wine Milestone

**What happened:** For the first time in modern history, French consumers drank more beer (22.1 million hectolitres) than wine (22.0 million hectolitres), according to Brasseurs de France and OIV data.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents the erosion of wine's cultural hegemony in its spiritual homeland. Craft brewing's expansion, lower price points, and casual positioning have made beer more accessible to younger French drinkers who reject the formality associated with wine culture.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands should note that casualisation, not premiumisation alone, drives adoption. Positioning that feels approachable and socially fluid—rather than aspirational or educational—may resonate more strongly with emerging demographics.

**What to monitor:** French Gen Z drinking occasion data; craft beer versus natural wine overlap in urban bistros; non-alcoholic beer adoption rates in traditionally wine-dominant markets.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Natural Wine as Social Identity in Shanghai

**What happened:** BUNCH, Shanghai's biannual natural wine festival, has grown from 500 to 800+ attendees since 2024, with founder Franklin Chiang noting that "people dress up for BUNCH" in ways they wouldn't for trade shows like Vinexpo.

**Why it is a signal:** Natural wine in China has transcended product category to become a marker of subcultural belonging. The emphasis on community, aesthetics, and "cool kids" positioning suggests that beverage choice functions as social signalling rather than taste preference.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands targeting urban Asian markets should consider event-based community building that prioritises aesthetic experience and social photography over education. The product becomes secondary to the social context it enables.

**What to monitor:** Natural wine event attendance demographics in Shanghai, Seoul, and Tokyo; crossover between fashion/art communities and beverage subcultures; emergence of non-alcoholic equivalents in these spaces.

### SIGNAL 3: Sliding-Scale Pricing as Hospitality Model

**What happened:** Sea & Soil Co-op, a worker-owned sandwich shop in Brooklyn, operates with sliding-scale pricing to make quality food accessible while fairly compensating staff.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a structural response to the "third space" accessibility crisis. Rather than accepting that quality hospitality requires exclusionary pricing, Sea & Soil demonstrates that alternative economic models can coexist with genuine quality.

**Societal layer:** Community / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brands and venues targeting values-driven consumers should explore transparent pricing models, cooperative structures, or pay-what-you-can options. Accessibility is becoming a brand value, not just a market segment.

**What to monitor:** Worker-owned hospitality ventures; consumer response to transparent pricing; intersection of food cooperatives and beverage innovation.

### SIGNAL 4: Regulatory Crackdown on Wellness-Washing Alcohol

**What happened:** The UK's Advertising Standards Authority banned a La Vieille Ferme TikTok ad that labelled wine boxes as "emotional support" and linked consumption to "self-care" and mental health.

**Why it is a signal:** Regulators are drawing sharper lines between alcohol marketing and wellbeing claims. The ruling specifically targeted language around mood improvement and therapeutic qualities—territory that alcohol brands have increasingly encroached upon.

**Societal layer:** Mental Health / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** This creates significant whitespace for non-alcoholic brands to authentically occupy wellbeing positioning that alcohol cannot claim. However, brands must ensure claims are substantiated—functional benefits require evidence, not just marketing language.

**What to monitor:** ASA and FTC rulings on alcohol wellness claims; functional beverage regulatory frameworks; consumer trust metrics for wellbeing-positioned drinks.

### SIGNAL 5: Premium Non-Alcoholic Earns Fine Dining Placement

**What happened:** Wild Idol, a naturally alcohol-free sparkling brand, is expanding into top bars, restaurants, and hotels, emphasising chef collaborations and single-vintage production as quality markers.

**Why it is a signal:** Non-alcoholic options are graduating from accommodation to aspiration. The emphasis on noble grape varieties, vintage specificity, and culinary pairing suggests that premium non-alcoholic can compete on craftsmanship rather than merely absence of alcohol.

**Societal layer:** Status / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Fine dining and premium hospitality venues should treat non-alcoholic pairings as a distinct revenue and experience opportunity, not a substitution. Brands should invest in sommelier education and chef partnerships to establish credibility.

**What to monitor:** Non-alcoholic wine list depth at Michelin-starred restaurants; sommelier certification programmes incorporating non-alcoholic; premium non-alcoholic price elasticity.

### SIGNAL 6: "Modern Australia" Wine Challenges Export Identity

**What happened:** The Uncorked: Modern Australia festival in Singapore brings 47 producers to challenge the Shiraz-and-Chardonnay narrative, emphasising restraint, freshness, and site expression.

**Why it is a signal:** Wine regions are actively rewriting their identities to align with contemporary preferences for lighter, more nuanced styles. The explicit rejection of "volume-driven" narratives suggests that provenance storytelling is shifting from scale to specificity.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands should consider how regional identity and terroir narratives—typically associated with wine—might translate to their category. Specificity of origin and process can differentiate in crowded markets.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to origin-specific non-alcoholic products; emergence of "natural" or "minimal intervention" positioning in non-alcoholic; Asian market receptivity to provenance narratives.

### SIGNAL 7: Japan's First Verified Low-Carbon Sake

**What happened:** Sakura Sake Shop launched 作 for 2126 (ZAKU for 2126), claiming to be Japan's first low-carbon sake with verified 30% emissions reduction per bottle, using no carbon offsets.

**Why it is a signal:** Sustainability claims are moving from vague aspiration to verified specificity. The use of product-level carbon footprint data and explicit rejection of offsets suggests that greenwashing tolerance is declining among conscious consumers.

**Societal layer:** Control / Spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Brands making sustainability claims must prepare for verification demands. Third-party carbon accounting and transparent methodology will become table stakes for credibility with values-driven consumers.

**What to monitor:** Consumer literacy around carbon offsets versus actual reduction; emergence of product-level carbon labelling standards; premium pricing tolerance for verified sustainability.

### SIGNAL 8: Rare Whisky Shifts to Quality Over Quantity

**What happened:** Despite difficult trading conditions, rare whisky releases continue—but with notably smaller quantities and more considered positioning, emphasising flavour and storytelling over famous distillery names.

**Why it is a signal:** The luxury spirits market is recalibrating from speculation-driven releases to genuine collector value. House of Hazelwood's emphasis on narrative over provenance suggests that even in premium categories, authenticity is displacing brand recognition.

**Societal layer:** Status / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium non-alcoholic releases should consider limited-edition, story-driven launches that create collector interest. Scarcity and narrative can generate value independent of alcohol content.

**What to monitor:** Secondary market activity for limited non-alcoholic releases; collector community emergence around premium non-alcoholic; crossover between whisky collectors and non-alcoholic enthusiasts.

### SIGNAL 10: English Wine's Competitive Emergence

**What happened:** English wines won the highest percentage of gold medals per entry at the International Wine Challenge, with 25 golds in 2026 versus 10 in 2025, and Kent emerging as the country's strongest region.

**Why it is a signal:** Climate change is reshaping wine geography, with previously marginal regions achieving quality recognition. This legitimises "unexpected origin" narratives and challenges traditional hierarchy assumptions.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands from unexpected origins should leverage the "emerging region" narrative that wine has established. Quality from surprising places carries novelty value and challenges assumptions.

**What to monitor:** English sparkling wine export growth; consumer receptivity to non-traditional wine regions; non-alcoholic production in emerging wine regions.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: Sliding-Scale Non-Alcoholic Bar Pop-Up

**Hypothesis:** A sliding-scale pricing model for premium non-alcoholic beverages will increase trial among price-sensitive consumers while maintaining profitability through voluntary higher payments from affluent customers, ultimately expanding the addressable market.

**Method:** Partner with an existing hospitality venue to operate a weekend pop-up featuring premium non-alcoholic cocktails and wines with suggested pricing tiers (e.g., £6/£9/£12 for the same drink). Track payment distribution, customer demographics, repeat visits, and social media sentiment. Include brief exit surveys on pricing perception and brand values alignment.

**Success metric:** Achieve average payment within 10% of standard pricing while increasing first-time trial by 40% compared to fixed-price control period; positive sentiment in 80%+ of social mentions referencing accessibility.

---

### Experiment 2: "Dress Code" Non-Alcoholic Tasting Event

**Hypothesis:** Positioning a non-alcoholic tasting event as a fashion-forward social occasion—where attendees are encouraged to dress up and the aesthetic experience is emphasised over education—will attract younger demographics who currently view non-alcoholic as functional rather than aspirational.

**Method:** Host a ticketed evening event in a visually distinctive venue, partnering with emerging fashion designers or stylists. Feature premium non-alcoholic wines and spirits with minimal educational content but strong visual presentation, DJ, and professional photography. Measure ticket sales, attendee demographics, social media engagement, and post-event purchase intent.

**Success metric:** 70%+ attendees under 35; 50%+ first-time premium non-alcoholic purchasers; event content achieves 3x engagement rate versus standard product photography.

---

### Experiment 3: Verified Carbon-Footprint Non-Alcoholic Launch

**Hypothesis:** A non-alcoholic beverage launched with third-party verified carbon footprint data and explicit "no offsets" positioning will command a 15-20% price premium among sustainability-conscious consumers and generate earned media coverage.

**Method:** Partner with a carbon accounting platform to measure and verify product-level emissions for a limited-edition non-alcoholic release. Create transparent packaging showing emissions data and methodology. Launch through sustainability-focused retailers and direct-to-consumer with A/B pricing tests. Track sales velocity, price elasticity, media coverage, and consumer feedback on credibility.

**Success metric:** Achieve 15% price premium with less than 10% volume decline versus non-verified comparable product; secure coverage in 3+ sustainability-focused publications; 85%+ positive sentiment on sustainability credibility in customer reviews.
