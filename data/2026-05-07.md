## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a hospitality industry caught between structural collapse and experiential reinvention. The UK pub sector is haemorrhaging venues at an accelerating rate—161 closures in Q1 2026, up 26% year-on-year—while simultaneously, venues that successfully pivot toward experience-led models are thriving. Balfour Winery's 30% year-on-year visitor growth demonstrates that consumers will travel and spend when offered more than mere consumption.

The mid-strength alcohol category is crystallising as a legitimate market segment, not a compromise. Olympic champion Jessica Ennis-Hill's launch of Seven Summers—a 7% dealcoholised Provence rosé—signals that moderation is moving from health necessity to lifestyle aspiration. Her framing of "balance" as athletic discipline applied to social life represents a significant cultural reframe: drinking less is now positioned as peak performance, not deprivation.

Connection anxiety continues to drive innovation. Teremana Tequila's "Share the Table" campaign and Downtime Bakery's Bakers' Hangs both respond to the same underlying tension—people crave in-person community but lack the infrastructure and permission structures to create it. Brands are increasingly becoming social architects, not just product providers.

The provenance narrative is fragmenting. While Bordeaux's established hierarchy maintains auction dominance, emerging regions like Bali and Indonesia's Luján de Cuyo are claiming legitimacy through distinctiveness rather than tradition. This "unapologetically local" positioning suggests consumers are increasingly receptive to origin stories that prioritise authenticity over prestige.

For non-alcoholic beverage strategists, the moment demands attention to three forces: the premiumisation of moderation, the venue as community infrastructure, and the growing appetite for products that facilitate connection rather than merely accompany it.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Athletic Framing of Moderation

**What happened:** Dame Jessica Ennis-Hill launched Seven Summers, a 7% ABV dealcoholised Provence rosé, explicitly positioning moderate drinking as an extension of athletic discipline and life balance rather than health restriction.

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a fundamental repositioning of reduced-alcohol consumption from compromise to optimisation. The involvement of a Master of Wine and premium Provence sourcing signals that the mid-strength category is ready for genuine premiumisation.

**Societal layer:** Control / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Mid-strength and no-alcohol products can now be marketed through performance and lifestyle optimisation narratives rather than health or sobriety messaging. Premium positioning requires premium provenance and production credentials.

**What to monitor:** Celebrity and athlete partnerships in the moderation space; consumer willingness to pay premium prices for reduced-alcohol products; emergence of "performance drinking" as a category descriptor.

---

### SIGNAL 2: Venue Collapse Meets Experience Ascendancy

**What happened:** UK pubs are closing at nearly two per day (161 in Q1 2026), yet Balfour Winery is tracking 30% visitor growth and expanding its experiential programming including yoga, art classes, and restaurant takeovers.

**Why it is a signal:** The divergence reveals that the crisis isn't about consumer appetite for hospitality—it's about the value proposition. Venues offering multi-dimensional experiences are thriving while single-purpose drinking establishments fail.

**Societal layer:** Community / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Beverage brands should consider venue partnerships that position their products within experiential contexts rather than traditional on-trade distribution. The "third place" is evolving into the "experience place."

**What to monitor:** Winery and brewery tourism metrics; hybrid venue concepts (pub-hotels, café-galleries); consumer time-spend data at hospitality venues versus purchase frequency.

---

### SIGNAL 3: Brands as Social Infrastructure

**What happened:** Teremana Tequila launched "Share the Table, Share the Mana"—a campaign partnering with social app Timeleft to host connection-focused events across 13 US cities, explicitly addressing the decline in in-person contact.

**Why it is a signal:** Beverage brands are moving beyond product marketing to actively constructing social infrastructure. The campaign acknowledges that consumers lack both the spaces and the permission structures for spontaneous connection.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic brands have a significant opportunity to own the "connection facilitator" positioning, particularly for occasions where alcohol might previously have served as social lubricant. Event programming becomes a core brand function.

**What to monitor:** Brand-hosted community events; partnerships between beverage companies and social/dating apps; consumer research on barriers to in-person socialising.

---

### SIGNAL 4: Craft Community as Antidote to Isolation

**What happened:** Downtime Bakery in Philadelphia has built its identity around "Bakers' Hangs"—informal gatherings where amateur and professional bakers swap treats and knowledge, addressing the isolation inherent in home-based craft work.

**Why it is a signal:** The cottage industry boom (accelerated by pandemic-era home production) has created a generation of isolated makers seeking peer community. This represents an underserved audience hungry for connection around shared craft.

**Societal layer:** Belonging / Identity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Functional beverages and non-alcoholic options can position as the drink of choice for craft communities—early morning bakers, home fermenters, makers of all kinds who need social fuel without impairment.

**What to monitor:** Growth in cottage food businesses; maker community platforms and events; beverage consumption patterns among creative/craft workers.

---

### SIGNAL 5: Unapologetic Regionalism

**What happened:** Baliwein is producing wines from Bali's volcanic vineyards that are "unapologetically Indonesian," while Luján de Cuyo DOC prepares its first UK masterclass to assert Argentine terroir identity.

**Why it is a signal:** Emerging wine regions are no longer seeking validation through comparison to European benchmarks. Instead, they're claiming legitimacy through distinctiveness and cultural authenticity.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Non-alcoholic beverages can leverage regional and cultural specificity as a differentiation strategy. "Unapologetically [origin]" positioning may resonate more than attempts to mimic established categories.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to unfamiliar origin stories; success of "anti-terroir" or "new terroir" marketing; regional ingredient sourcing trends in NA beverages.

---

### SIGNAL 6: The Cooperative Resurgence

**What happened:** Champagne H. Blin, a 75-year-old cooperative in Vallée de la Marne, is gaining recognition for championing the Meunier grape, demonstrating how collective structures can preserve heritage while achieving scale.

**Why it is a signal:** In an era of consolidation and corporate ownership, cooperative models offer an alternative narrative of community ownership and heritage preservation that resonates with values-driven consumers.

**Societal layer:** Community / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Cooperative and community-owned production models could differentiate non-alcoholic brands, particularly those emphasising local ingredients or traditional methods. Ownership structure becomes part of the brand story.

**What to monitor:** Consumer awareness of and preference for cooperative-produced goods; emergence of NA beverage cooperatives; B-Corp and community ownership trends in beverage.

---

### SIGNAL 7: UPF Anxiety Reaches Clinical Consensus

**What happened:** Cardiologist groups issued a "clinical consensus statement" advising patients to cook more at home, avoid late-night eating, and chew slowly to reduce ultra-processed food intake.

**Why it is a signal:** Medical establishment endorsement of UPF concerns moves the issue from wellness culture to mainstream health guidance, potentially accelerating consumer scrutiny of processed beverages.

**Societal layer:** Control / Mental Health

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Ingredient transparency and minimal processing will become increasingly important purchase drivers. "Clean label" positioning in NA beverages moves from differentiator to table stakes.

**What to monitor:** Regulatory attention to UPF in beverages; consumer label-reading behaviour; growth of "whole ingredient" beverage positioning.

---

### SIGNAL 8: The Premiumisation of Convenience

**What happened:** Mionetto launched an Orange Spritz Aperitivo RTS (ready-to-serve) in both alcoholic and alcohol-free versions, while Quince expanded its DTC platform to include wine, caviar, and specialty beverages.

**Why it is a signal:** Convenience formats are shedding their downmarket associations. Consumers increasingly expect premium quality in ready-to-consume formats, creating space for sophisticated NA RTD products.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / Status

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Premium NA RTD products can command higher price points if they deliver on quality credentials and occasion appropriateness. Format innovation should not compromise positioning.

**What to monitor:** Price elasticity in premium RTD category; consumer perception research on RTD quality; growth of single-serve premium formats.

---

### SIGNAL 9: Scarcity as Social Catalyst

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia's only licensed alcohol shop is experiencing shortages due to regional conflict, creating long queues and social tension among foreign residents seeking access.

**Why it is a signal:** Scarcity transforms consumption into community experience—queuing becomes social ritual, access becomes status marker. This dynamic has implications for how limited-availability products create community.

**Societal layer:** Status / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Limited releases and controlled scarcity can build community and desirability for NA brands, but must be managed carefully to avoid exclusion. "Drop culture" mechanics may translate to beverage.

**What to monitor:** Limited-release strategies in beverage; queue culture and social dynamics around scarce products; membership and allocation models.

---

### SIGNAL 10: The Sustainability-Productivity Convergence

**What happened:** AB InBev opened a wastewater treatment facility that generates biogas to power brewery operations, framing environmental investment as operational efficiency rather than pure CSR.

**Why it is a signal:** Sustainability initiatives are being repositioned from cost centres to productivity drivers, making environmental credentials more defensible in challenging economic conditions.

**Societal layer:** Control / Spirituality

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA beverage brands can frame sustainability investments as quality and efficiency improvements rather than premium-justifying costs. Circular economy narratives gain commercial credibility.

**What to monitor:** Consumer response to efficiency-framed sustainability messaging; operational sustainability innovations in beverage production; B2B sustainability requirements from hospitality buyers.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Moderation Masterclass Series

**Hypothesis:** Consumers interested in reduced-alcohol options will pay premium prices for educational experiences that position moderation as sophisticated connoisseurship rather than restriction.

**Method:** Partner with a Master of Wine or certified sommelier to develop a four-session "Art of Moderation" masterclass series covering mid-strength wines, dealcoholised spirits, and premium NA beverages. Host in aspirational venues (winery tasting rooms, boutique hotels). Price at £75-100 per session to test premium positioning. Include take-home product samples and tasting notes.

**Success metric:** 80% sell-through rate; post-session purchase intent above 60%; Net Promoter Score above 50; participant willingness to pay premium (>£15) for featured NA products.

---

### Experiment 2: The Maker's Morning Social

**Hypothesis:** Home-based craft workers (bakers, fermenters, makers) represent an underserved community seeking peer connection, and will adopt a functional NA beverage brand that facilitates early-morning social gatherings.

**Method:** Identify three cities with active cottage food/maker communities. Partner with local bakeries or maker spaces to host monthly 7am "Maker's Morning" gatherings featuring a branded functional NA beverage (adaptogenic coffee alternative or botanical tonic). Provide conversation prompts and skill-sharing structure. Document community formation and beverage consumption patterns over six months.

**Success metric:** Average attendance growth of 20% month-over-month; organic community formation (attendees organising independent gatherings); 40% of attendees purchasing featured beverage for home use; social media community engagement.

---

### Experiment 3: The Unapologetic Origin Story

**Hypothesis:** Non-alcoholic beverages with strong, distinctive regional identity will outperform category-generic products when marketed through "unapologetically [origin]" positioning.

**Method:** Develop two parallel marketing campaigns for the same NA botanical beverage: one emphasising category benefits (refreshing, sophisticated, alcohol-free) and one emphasising specific regional origin story (local botanicals, traditional preparation methods, cultural context). A/B test across matched digital audiences and measure engagement, click-through, and conversion. Follow up with qualitative research on brand perception and purchase intent.

**Success metric:** Origin-story campaign achieves 25% higher engagement rate; 15% higher conversion rate; qualitative research shows stronger brand recall and emotional connection; willingness to pay premium increases by 20% for origin-positioned product.
