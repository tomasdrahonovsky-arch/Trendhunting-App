## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a fundamental tension between optimisation culture and the human need for unquantified pleasure. The viral backlash against Steven Bartlett's lament over three days "ruined" by a single glass of wine crystallises a growing exhaustion with biohacking orthodoxy—people are pushing back against the idea that every moment must be measured, tracked, and maximised. This creates significant white space for beverages positioned around intentional indulgence rather than functional benefit.

Simultaneously, the drinks industry is undergoing a format revolution. RTDs have achieved 17% value growth by solving for convenience and decision fatigue—the same friction-removal that Spotify brought to music discovery. Meanwhile, celebrity wine brands like Kylie Minogue's are proving that emotional resonance and identity signalling can drive category leadership, with her no/low sparkling rosé capturing 44% of total category growth.

The experiential layer of drinking culture is being reimagined. From Hong Kong's Poco Vino tram activations integrating with yoga studios and running clubs, to Yorkshire's Honley Wine Bar using Coravin technology to transform hesitant consumers into adventurous drinkers, the industry is learning that transformation—not transaction—builds loyalty. Berlin's nightlife is shifting from cold exclusivity toward warmer, more accessible experiences, signalling broader hospitality recalibration.

For non-alcoholic beverage builders, the strategic imperative is clear: stop competing on health claims alone. The winning position lies at the intersection of ritual, identity, and permission—giving consumers sophisticated options that feel like celebration rather than compromise. The brands that understand drinking as a social and emotional technology, not merely a functional one, will capture the emerging demand.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: The Anti-Optimisation Backlash

**What happened:** Steven Bartlett's viral complaint about three days "ruined" by one glass of wine sparked widespread mockery, with commentators celebrating the "joyless optimisation freaks" having a "bad few days."

**Why it is a signal:** This represents a cultural inflection point where the biohacking, WHOOP-tracking, productivity-maximising lifestyle is being actively rejected. The pendulum is swinging from quantified self toward qualified experience.

**Societal layer:** Pleasure / rebellion

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Position premium NA drinks as tools for intentional indulgence rather than health optimisation. Marketing that emphasises "permission to enjoy" rather than "guilt-free" will resonate with consumers fatigued by wellness metrics.

**What to monitor:** Sentiment shifts in wellness communities; growth of "anti-productivity" content creators; decline in wearable tech engagement among younger demographics.

### SIGNAL 2: RTD Format Revolution

**What happened:** Ready-to-drink beverages achieved 17% value growth and 12% volume growth, generating £704 million in UK off-trade sales, with 44% of sales coming from consumers shifting spend from spirits.

**Why it is a signal:** This confirms that convenience and decision-reduction are now primary purchase drivers. Consumers want quality without cognitive load—the same dynamic that transformed music and banking.

**Societal layer:** Control / pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Accelerate RTD format development for NA cocktails and functional beverages. Pre-mixed, single-serve formats with clear flavour profiles will outperform complex, DIY-required products.

**What to monitor:** Canned cocktail innovation; supermarket shelf space allocation; subscription box adoption rates for RTD products.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: The Anti-Optimisation NA Collection

**Hypothesis:** Consumers fatigued by wellness metrics will respond to NA beverages positioned around "intentional indulgence" rather than health benefits, with messaging that explicitly rejects optimisation culture.

**Method:** Develop a limited-edition NA cocktail range with names and packaging that playfully reject biohacking culture (e.g., "Untracked," "Off-Grid," "No Data"). Launch through lifestyle venues and fitness studio partnerships with messaging focused on "moments worth not measuring." Track purchase motivation through post-purchase surveys comparing "health" vs. "pleasure" drivers.

**Success metric:** 40% of purchasers cite "permission to enjoy" or similar pleasure-focused language as primary purchase driver; 25% social sharing rate with anti-optimisation messaging.

---

### Experiment 2: NA Connoisseurship Programme

**Hypothesis:** Structured tasting education for NA beverages will increase consumer confidence, vocabulary development, and willingness to trade up to premium products—replicating the transformation model proven at Honley Wine Bar.

**Method:** Partner with three independent venues to launch monthly NA tasting flights with guided education, rotating through botanical spirits, dealcoholised wines, and functional aperitifs. Provide tasting notes and flavour wheel vocabulary. Track participant purchase behaviour over six months, measuring average transaction value and category exploration breadth.

**Success metric:** 30% increase in average transaction value among programme participants; 50% of participants purchase from three or more NA subcategories within six months.

---

### Experiment 3: Lifestyle Integration Partnership Ecosystem

**Hypothesis:** NA beverages integrated into existing community rituals (fitness, creative, wellness) will achieve higher brand recall and purchase intent than standalone marketing activations.

**Method:** Develop six-month partnership programme with running clubs, yoga studios, and creative co-working spaces in two test markets. Provide branded NA beverages for post-activity consumption, with no hard selling—only presence and sampling. Measure brand recall, purchase intent, and actual purchase behaviour against control group exposed to traditional digital advertising at equivalent spend.

**Success metric:** 2x brand recall versus control; 35% higher purchase intent; 20% conversion to retail purchase within 90 days.
