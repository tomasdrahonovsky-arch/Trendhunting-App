## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Today's cultural landscape reveals a profound tension between authenticity and manipulation, between heritage and reinvention. The most significant through-line is the erosion of trust in systems—from streaming charts allegedly gamed by prediction market traders to AI-generated child abuse imagery forcing parents to retreat from public sharing. Consumers are navigating a world where nothing can be taken at face value.

In beverages, the "mid-strength" wine category (6-8.5% ABV) is crystallising as a legitimate segment, driven not by explicit health messaging but by celebrity endorsement and lifestyle positioning. Jessica Ennis-Hill's Seven Summers launch articulates the value proposition clearly: "balance" and "lighter for modern life." Critically, consumers aren't consciously choosing based on ABV—they're choosing based on identity alignment. This suggests the non-alcoholic and low-alcohol space must continue moving away from deficit framing ("what's missing") toward aspiration framing ("who I become").

The UK's incoming Prime Minister pledging a 20% business rates cut for pubs signals potential hospitality revival, but the underlying economics remain brutal. Venues need differentiated offerings that justify premium pricing and drive footfall beyond traditional drinking occasions.

Meanwhile, the Paris heatwave forcing fashion shows to 9am start times and France recording 2,025 excess deaths in a single week underscores how climate is reshaping every aspect of social gathering—including when, where, and what we drink. Functional hydration and temperature-appropriate beverages become infrastructure, not indulgence.

The cultural moment demands brands that can demonstrate verified authenticity (B Corp certification cutting through "sustainability literacy" gaps), offer genuine community (not algorithmically manufactured engagement), and provide refuge from an increasingly synthetic world.

## TREND SIGNALS

### SIGNAL 1: Mid-Strength Wine Emerges as Identity Category

**What happened:** Sainsbury's created the UK's first dedicated mid-strength wine bay, while celebrities including Kylie Minogue (7% ABV rosé) and Olympic champion Jessica Ennis-Hill (Seven Summers, 7% dealcoholised Provence rosé at £12.50) are legitimising the 6-8.5% ABV segment.

**Why it is a signal:** Consumers aren't consciously selecting wines by ABV—they're buying into lifestyle positioning. Ennis-Hill's framing of "balance" and "lighter for modern life" shifts the category from compromise to aspiration. The segment has existed quietly for years (Barefoot White Zinfandel at 8%) but is now being actively merchandised and celebrity-endorsed.

**Societal layer:** Identity / Control

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** The success of mid-strength validates the "moderation spectrum" rather than binary alcohol/no-alcohol positioning. NA brands should consider how they sit within a broader portfolio of reduced-alcohol options, and whether retail partnerships could create "mindful drinking" bays that include NA alongside mid-strength.

**What to monitor:** Sainsbury's mid-strength bay sales data; whether other UK retailers follow; celebrity beverage launches in the 5-9% ABV range; consumer language around "balance" versus "health."

### SIGNAL 2: Algorithmic Manipulation Erodes Trust in Cultural Charts

**What happened:** WIRED reported that Kalshi prediction market traders allegedly deployed streaming bots to inflate Malcolm Todd's "Earrings" on Spotify, manipulating charts to win bets. Spotify removed 500,000+ streams. Artists including Russ condemned the practice, noting artists may not know if their success is genuine.

**Why it is a signal:** When cultural success metrics become financial instruments, they become targets for manipulation. This extends beyond music—any popularity metric (trending products, viral moments, "best-seller" lists) is now suspect. Consumers and creators alike are losing faith in organic discovery.

**Societal layer:** Control / Authenticity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** "Best-selling" and "trending" claims carry diminishing weight. Brands must demonstrate authenticity through verifiable, harder-to-fake signals: local community endorsement, transparent sourcing stories, third-party certifications. Consider how to create "proof of genuine popularity" that resists manipulation.

**What to monitor:** Expansion of prediction markets into food/beverage categories; consumer scepticism toward "trending" claims; growth of verification/provenance technologies.

### SIGNAL 3: B Corp as Literacy Shortcut for Sustainability

**What happened:** Cork Supply's sustainability director stated that B Corp certification helps overcome "a lack of literacy around sustainability" because complex environmental issues lack clear causality, making people "doubt." B Corp serves as a trusted shorthand that "cuts through the noise."

**Why it is a signal:** Sustainability fatigue is real—consumers want to make good choices but lack the expertise to evaluate competing claims. Third-party certifications that are widely recognised and commercially integrated (not siloed in a "sustainability department") become essential trust proxies.

**Societal layer:** Control / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** For NA brands making sustainability claims, certification matters more than self-reported metrics. B Corp, organic, and similar marks provide the "literacy shortcut" consumers need. Brands without certification should prioritise obtaining it or find equally credible verification mechanisms.

**What to monitor:** Consumer recognition rates of various sustainability certifications; B Corp growth in beverage categories; emergence of new certification schemes specific to beverages.

### SIGNAL 4: Climate Reshapes Social Gathering Infrastructure

**What happened:** Paris Fashion Week was disrupted by 35°C+ temperatures, with Dior moving its show to 9am. France recorded 2,025 excess deaths (29% increase) during the hottest week of June's record heatwave, with authorities expecting the toll to rise.

**Why it is a signal:** Extreme heat is no longer exceptional—it's structural. Social gatherings, hospitality venues, and consumption occasions must adapt. Morning and late-evening windows become premium; midday becomes dead time. Hydration and cooling become functional necessities, not lifestyle choices.

**Societal layer:** Community / Pleasure

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Develop products and occasions explicitly designed for heat resilience. Morning social drinking occasions (9am fashion shows suggest appetite for early gathering) could feature sophisticated NA options. Functional hydration with electrolytes, cooling botanicals, or temperature-specific formats (frozen, extra-cold) become infrastructure rather than novelty.

**What to monitor:** Hospitality venue operating hour shifts; growth of morning/early evening social occasions; functional hydration product launches; climate-adaptive venue design.

### SIGNAL 5: Parental Retreat from Public Digital Sharing

**What happened:** The UK's National Crime Agency and Internet Watch Foundation issued landmark guidance urging parents not to post children's photos publicly online, citing AI-generated sexual abuse material. They recommend private accounts or "close friends" sharing only.

**Why it is a signal:** The default of public sharing is reversing. Parents—a key demographic for family-oriented hospitality—are retreating to private, trusted circles. This accelerates the shift from broadcast social media to intimate group sharing and closed communities.

**Societal layer:** Control / Belonging

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** Marketing strategies relying on user-generated content featuring families face headwinds. Brands should facilitate private sharing (exclusive groups, closed communities) rather than expecting public endorsement. Family-friendly venues might offer "no-photo" experiences as a feature, not a limitation.

**What to monitor:** Decline in public family content on Instagram/TikTok; growth of private sharing apps; parental attitudes toward brand photography policies at venues.

### SIGNAL 6: Heritage and Provenance as Competitive Moat

**What happened:** Château Sainte Marguerite emphasised its 50-year family ownership, full estate control, and status as the only Provence cru classé with both organic and vegan certifications (since 2003). Separately, 3,000+ bottles of Alta Langa were stolen from Banfi—thieves "knew exactly what they were doing" in selecting specific prestigious wines.

**Why it is a signal:** In a world of algorithmic manipulation and synthetic authenticity, deep heritage and verified provenance become irreplaceable assets. The wine theft demonstrates that criminals recognise the value of authentic prestige—it cannot be counterfeited.

**Societal layer:** Status / Authenticity

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands should invest in building genuine heritage stories now, even if young. Document founding stories, preserve archives, maintain founder involvement. Provenance traceability (as Banfi uses) could become a consumer-facing feature, not just supply chain infrastructure.

**What to monitor:** Consumer willingness to pay premiums for verified provenance; blockchain/traceability adoption in beverages; growth of "founder-led" brand narratives.

**Why it is a signal:** Geopolitical instability is forcing beverage brands to diversify markets rapidly. Asia represents growth opportunity but requires cultural adaptation. This creates openings for brands (including NA) that can navigate cross-cultural positioning.

**Societal layer:** Status / Community

**Implication for non-alcoholic beverages / hospitality:** NA brands with US or European heritage should explore Asian market entry while trade routes are being redrawn. The "Discover US Spirits" model—trade missions, buyer tastings, hospitality professional engagement—could be adapted for NA category building in new markets.

**What to monitor:** Asian consumer attitudes toward Western NA brands; trade mission programmes for non-alcoholic categories; regulatory environments for NA beverages in Japan, Taiwan, South Korea.

## TOP 3 EXPERIMENTS

### Experiment 1: "Verified Popularity" Transparency Campaign

**Hypothesis:** In an era of algorithmic manipulation and fake metrics, consumers will reward brands that transparently demonstrate genuine (not manufactured) popularity through verifiable, hard-to-fake signals.

**Method:** Launch a campaign for an NA beverage brand that publicly shares real-time, third-party-verified sales data, customer reviews from verified purchasers only, and geographic distribution of actual customers (not bots). Partner with a blockchain provenance platform to create an immutable record. Contrast explicitly with "trending" claims that cannot be verified. Test in one market against a control market using standard marketing claims.

**Success metric:** 15% higher purchase intent in test market; 25% higher brand trust scores; earned media coverage positioning brand as "authenticity leader"; measurable social conversation around verification/transparency.

---

### Experiment 2: Climate-Adaptive "Morning Social" Occasion Creation

**Hypothesis:** As extreme heat reshapes daily rhythms (evidenced by Paris Fashion Week's 9am show times), there is latent demand for sophisticated morning social occasions featuring premium NA beverages—a "third space" between breakfast and lunch.

**Method:** Partner with three hospitality venues in heat-affected cities (e.g., London, Barcelona, Austin) to pilot "Morning Social" programming from 9-11am during summer months. Curate a menu of complex, coffee-alternative NA beverages (botanical infusions, sparkling teas, adaptogenic drinks) positioned as social rather than functional. Include light food pairings. Market as "beat the heat" social occasions for remote workers, creatives, and those seeking connection outside evening drinking hours.

**Success metric:** 50+ attendees per session; 40% repeat attendance; positive unit economics (beverage + food margin covers venue costs); qualitative feedback indicating "this filled a gap I didn't know existed."

---

### Experiment 3: "Closed Circle" Private Community Platform

**Hypothesis:** As parents retreat from public sharing and consumers seek trusted micro-communities, an NA brand that facilitates private, invitation-only community spaces will build deeper loyalty than one relying on public social media engagement.

**Method:** Create a private, app-based community for an NA brand's most engaged customers (top 1,000 by purchase frequency). Offer exclusive content, early product access, and facilitated small-group connections (e.g., matching members in same city for meetups). Explicitly position as "no public sharing required"—what happens in the community stays there. Measure engagement, retention, and program economics against equivalent investment in public social media marketing.

**Success metric:** 60% monthly active rate among community members; 2x purchase frequency versus non-member customers; Net Promoter Score 20+ points higher than brand average; successful member-organised meetups in 5+ cities without brand coordination.
